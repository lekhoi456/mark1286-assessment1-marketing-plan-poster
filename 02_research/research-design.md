# Research design: Marketing Plan Poster

Status (28 September 2026): the group selected Green SM (D-018); focus market Copenhagen, Denmark (D-031); segment S1, positioning P1 and a DKK600,000 12-month market-entry budget (D-032). Four-market evidence research, strategy alternatives, module mapping and the reproducible budget/KPI model are complete. This is the working method, not an extra methodology panel on the poster. The brief and rubric govern assessed content; research devices stay behind the scenes (D-013). Line items, channels, assumptions, targets, brand message and innovation mechanism remain group approvals.

## 1. Purpose and unit of analysis

Develop one coherent marketing plan for one specified business/product/service, target market and planning period. Link customer evidence to positioning, brand, acquisition and sales, costs and measurable business objectives. The output is an A0 poster and 15-minute group pitch, not a long report.

Approach: an applied, document-based case study with deductive use of the module's concepts and explicit comparison of strategic alternatives. This is not an experiment or a representative consumer survey; do not claim causal impact or population representativeness from secondary sources.

## 3. Case selection before commitment (P2)

If the group already has a case, evaluate it against the gates and criteria; do not pretend a shortlist was considered. If no case is selected, ask only for group preferences and constraints, then research a manageable shortlist. Freeze the scoring rules before scoring named candidates.

**Applied cutover:** the generic business-screen criteria below are the preserved P0 proposal, not the later focus-market calculation. The group directly chose Green SM, so no fictitious business shortlist was scored. `business-selection/focus-market-selection.md` and `market-selection-inputs.json` record the separate K1–K6 **25/20/20/15/10/10** focus-market method frozen before that comparison. Its numerical anchors/European preference operationalisation belong to the agent; the group's broad preference is D-023. Do not combine the two weight systems or claim group approval of numerical thresholds.

### Mandatory gates

1. A real or explicitly hypothetical business, with a clearly bounded product/service, country and customer decision.
2. Enough accessible, verifiable evidence to address the brief without fabricated results. Hypothetical businesses still need sourced market context; their internal figures remain assumptions.
3. Work can be researched, understood and presented before the actual pitch date.
4. Private business information is used only with permission and a clear plan for what may appear in the assessed poster.
5. The group is willing to defend the case. Lecturer approval is a gate only if required locally (Q-L9), not an invented brief rule.

A failed gate is not offset by a high weighted score. Unknown evidence is recorded as unknown, not assigned a plausible score.

### Proposed weighted criteria (group approval at P2)

| Criterion | Weight | Score 1 | Score 3 | Score 5 |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Evidence availability and verifiability | 25 | Critical claims inaccessible | Core facts available, some material gaps | Strong primary and independent evidence, PDFs obtainable |
| Fit across the eleven elements and rubric | 25 | Several elements speculative | All elements workable, uneven depth | Clear connected decisions across the brief |
| Customer and competitive decision clarity | 20 | Diffuse audience/problem | Bounded audience with limited comparisons | Prioritisation and credible competitor trade-offs |
| Budget, sales and KPI feasibility | 15 | Costs/process ungrounded | Indicative inputs and transparent assumptions | Feasible cost basis, resources and measurable process |
| Meaningful emerging-concept application | 10 | Buzzword only | Plausible application with constraints | Distinct mechanism tied to customer/business value |
| Group knowledge and information permission | 5 | Little usable access | Public knowledge sufficient | Relevant confirmed knowledge with permission |
| Total | 100 | | | |

Scores are ordinal judgements, not scientific precision. Weighted total = sum(weight × score / 5), out of 100. Each score needs a reason and evidence IDs/source leads. Separate evidence confidence from suitability; a high score with low confidence requires further collection.

### Sensitivity and decision rule

- Move each weight up and down by 20% of its base value one at a time, renormalise all weights to 100 and recalculate rankings.
- Repeat with equal weights; vary genuinely uncertain scores by ±1 within 1–5.
- Record the winner, runner-up, margin and any rank reversal. Do not invent probabilities for ordinal scores.
- If the result reverses under plausible changes, call the choice sensitive and identify the decisive missing evidence. The group chooses explicitly; do not change weights after seeing results without logging why.

## 4. Evidence collection and claim status

| Source type | Use | Minimum treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Business primary sources | Offer, locations, prices, existing operations | PDF, date, locator; company claims are attributed, not independently validated by repetition |
| Official statistics / robust research | Country, market, consumers | Figure year, geography, population, unit and method; publication date is not data year |
| Independent reporting / research | Central need, competitor context, feasibility | Check independence from the same press release or study |
| Module slides and original academic works | Concepts and application | Module register with exact wording; cite the work actually read |
| Group inputs | Permissions, decisions and actual contributions | Dated confirmation; never fabricated observations |
| Perplexity / Study Hub | Discovery and learning | Leads only, never cited |

Central strategic claims should have two substantively independent sources, including one independent of the business. If this is unavailable, narrow or qualify the claim and expose the limitation. Two sites reproducing one report count as one evidence origin.

Each factual claim goes into `evidence-log.md`, linked to the PDF registry. Distinguish observed fact, source-reported opinion, derived calculation, planning assumption and proposed target. Verification of a source is not verification that a claim follows from it.

## 5. Analysis procedures (P3–P4)

1. Establish scope: offer, country, customer segment, period, business objective and exclusions.
2. Compare segment alternatives using customer need, reachability, willingness/ability to pay and fit with capacity. Use module concepts only where they improve the decision.
3. Compare competitors on meaningful attributes with equivalent units and dates; avoid unsupported perceptual-map scores or claims of 'unique'/'first'.
4. Form a positioning statement and demonstrate the link to brand identity and channel choices.
5. Draw the sales process: lead generation, qualification/prospecting, conversion and follow-up; identify who does what and the implementation constraint.
6. Model budget in one currency and period; reconcile cost × quantity, line totals, total and percentage allocation. Separate marketing spend from revenue. Record tax, exchange-rate and staff-time assumptions if material.
7. Derive scenario outcomes from labelled inputs. For a funnel, keep denominators and periods consistent; multiplication illustrates a scenario, not a forecast guaranteed to occur. Stress the inputs that change the recommendation.
8. Define each KPI's objective, unit, baseline if known, proposed target, tracking tool, review frequency and response rule. Unknown baselines remain unknown.
9. Test an emerging concept's mechanism, customer benefit, resource cost, data/privacy implications and a simpler alternative. Do not force every example trend in the brief into one plan.
10. Integrate objectives, tactics, costs and KPIs. Reject contradictions before compressing the plan into panels.

## 6. Quality and review

- Credibility: triangulate central claims, document conflicts, inspect PDFs and original locators.
- Dependability: frozen selection criteria, dated extraction, versioned models/drafts, decision trail.
- Confirmability: claim IDs, saved originals, reproducible arithmetic and explicit assumptions.
- Independent critique: two reviewers receive the actual brief/rubric and draft, but not the writer's preferred score. One uses a local-marker lens, one a UK-moderator lens; these are mock roles, not actual markers.
- No official band descriptors are available in the supplied A1 rubric. Proposed top-band tests and mock marks are reasoned estimates, never claimed official standards or guaranteed grades.

## 7. Phase gates and limitations

Business/segment/positioning/budget/design decisions belong to the group. Explain phases and preserve their approval gates. Under D-027, independent research, alternative preparation and assumption-labelled calculations can continue without overnight questions; they do not adopt a strategy or authorise spending. The evidence base remains secondary and geographically uneven. Exact limits, lecturer AI-use requirements and truthful contributions remain explicit controls.
