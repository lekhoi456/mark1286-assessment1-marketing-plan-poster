# Age segmentation options for panel 2

Prepared: 3 October 2026. Authority: D-042/LG-003, the student's report that the local lecturer requires age segmentation. This note preserves the age-choice decision support. **Update D-043: the student approved the priority range 25–44.** Exact poster wording/visuals and any budget/KPI revision are not adopted here.

## Correction to the earlier proposal

**Adults 18+** states a lower age threshold. It does not show how age groups were compared or selected. Retain the approved S1 buying-situation definition, but refine its age scope through the student's decision. The request does not itself select an age band.

Working age groups: **18–24; 25–44; 45–64; 65+**. Proposed campaign priority: **25–44**, limited to residents in the usable Copenhagen service area who already take recurring local taxi trips and pay personally. The other groups are comparison groups, not proven inferior customers or ineligible service users. No secondary campaign, age-specific budget or new commercial target is adopted.

The proposal is a manageable age scope for the existing S1 campaign, supported by the largest of these adult population groups and qualitative channel evidence. Confidence in the exact targeting choice is **moderate**. City counts and operator testimony do not establish age-specific taxi spending, repeat use, acquisition cost or willingness to switch. Confidence in the checked population arithmetic is **high**.

**25–44:** sum ages 25 through 44 = **269,277**; 269,277 ÷ 670,389 × 100 = **40.1673…%**, displayed as **40.2%**. These are residents, not taxi users or the eligible campaign audience. The figure does not count people meeting S1's behaviour/payer/service-area conditions.

The JSON records all 126 single-year values, source-PDF hash, mutually exclusive age groups and reconciliation checks. Age rows sum exactly to 670,389; the bands including 0–17 sum to that same total. Children are excluded from campaign targeting, not from the population denominator.

## Taxi booking-channel evidence and limits

`konkurrence-og-forbrugerstyrelsen-2026b`, PDF p. 85, paragraph 332, reports an operator's statement:

> En aktør har således oplyst, at yngre og mere prisfølsomme kunder primært bestiller taxikørsel via app, mens ældre kunder i højere grad foretrækker personlig betjening og telefon-

The next line continues **bestilling**. Translation for analysis: one operator reported that younger, more price-sensitive customers mainly book taxis through an app, while older customers more often prefer personal service and telephone booking. This is operator testimony reproduced in the authority decision, not an age-specific customer-survey result. It gives no numerical age boundaries, and does not prove that all customers aged 25–44 prefer apps or all older customers prefer telephone booking.

Existing income context: `statistics-denmark-2025`, PDF p. 2, reports Copenhagen's 2024 average disposable income as DKK139,995 for ages 20–24, DKK226,890 for 25–29 and DKK307,601 for 30–34. City averages are not an individual's income or proof of taxi affordability. These do not need to appear on the infographic; no income threshold, occupation or household type is invented.

Connect that selected band to the resident-rider illustration; retain the geographic, payer and repeat-taxi-use filters and the three profile lenses. Prefer a small population context badge over interpreting 40.2% as a share of taxi customers.

Age priority and exact display wording remain for discussion.
