## 2. Three meaningful segments, applied to both cities

Segmentation follows `market-segmentation` and `stp-model` (`w04-lecture` s7), `segmentation-bases` (`w04-tutorial` s5; `w04-lecture` s6), and `targeting` (`w04-lecture` s7). The primary division is **buying situation and payer**, then geography and behavioural frequency. Demographics describe relevant people but do not substitute for evidence of need. The three segments are alternatives for priority, not three simultaneous launches.

### S1 — Resident, self-paid taxi users on repeat local occasions

**P definition:** adults living in the verified service area who already choose taxis for some recurring trips and pay themselves. “Repeat” is an eligibility question to validate, not a claim about the general population. No arbitrary age ceiling, gender or affluent-professional persona is imposed.

**Why consider S1 first?** I: it links the strongest Danish customer evidence to the documented local-stability objective and provides a repeat-use question that marketing can test. For Amsterdam it is a geographically manageable hypothesis, but weaker consumer evidence makes validation more important. It is not proven that S1 is the largest or most profitable segment.

### S2 — Locally based organisations buying discretionary staff/guest trips

**P definition:** organisations within confirmed service coverage buying ordinary taxi trips for staff or guests, rather than public-sector contract transport, patient transport or school transport. Demographic description applies to adult riders and purchasing roles; organisation size, sector, trip volume and payer are business segmentation variables. Do not invent purchasing-manager ages or genders.

- **F anchors:** DK-26–DK-27 document operating model and historical coverage, not corporate account demand. NL `ilt-nd-marktbeeld-taxi.pdf` p. 2 reports a national 80% contract-market share in its 2022 market picture, but the denominator is unspecified and the category must not be equated with discretionary corporate taxi accounts. The Dutch pilot's office and recruitment are documented in the NL “Pilot promotion…” row; a functioning account-sales offer is not established by these facts.
- **P psychographics/needs:** a buyer may value a clear contact, booking responsibility, readable trip records and service resolution. These are research hypotheses, not reported customer requests. **P behaviour:** a named administrator approves trips, riders use the service, and the organisation pays; actual authorisation, invoicing and repeat demand remain U.
- **P reachability:** identify organisations inside coverage using legitimate public business information; invite a discovery conversation through permitted channels; offer a limited paid trial only after service and payment capability are verified. Proposed direct selling is not an existing Green SM corporate programme in Denmark or the Netherlands.
- **U ability to pay:** approved travel budgets, fare ceilings, procurement cycle, invoicing requirements and incumbent contracts. Company income or aggregate taxi-market size cannot answer these questions.
- **Boundary:** sales must not promise priority dispatch, guaranteed availability, consolidated billing, reporting integrations or service credits unless deliverable and costed. A manually reviewed trip record is a possible proposal, not evidence of an available system. Keep personal trip detail away from employers unless its disclosure is appropriate and legally verified.
- **Counter-argument:** longer selling cycles and account support consume limited staff time; established suppliers may already provide the same benefits. The stronger individual-user evidence in Denmark does not prove business-buyer demand. Amsterdam's national contract-market statistic could mislead the plan into an unsuitable segment.
- **Validation:** interview actual decision-makers and riders separately, request procurement criteria voluntarily, inspect required trip geography/times, confirm payment operations, and compare named incumbent account offers. Reject S2 as the priority if the service cannot meet buying requirements or the available period cannot accommodate the sales cycle.

### S3 — Short-stay visitors choosing an in-city taxi; airport transfer as a separate gated extension

**P definition:** adult visitors with a taxi-appropriate trip inside verified coverage; hotel/venue staff may influence the booking, but the visitor remains the end customer. International/domestic, business/leisure and party/luggage characteristics are research dimensions, not a fabricated single tourist persona.

- **F anchors:** Copenhagen airport passengers and Capital Region overnight stays are DK-11–DK-12. Amsterdam hotel guests are in the NL “Amsterdam received 9.5 million…” row, `cbs-2026-gasten-logiesaccommodaties.pdf` p. 3; Schiphol O&D passengers and travel purposes are `royal-schiphol-group-nd-traffic-review-2025.pdf` pp. 21, 26. Passenger movements, guests and nights are different units; none is a count of obtainable taxi customers.
- **P psychographic/behavioural hypothesis:** unfamiliarity with a city may make understandable pick-up information, fare terms and a human help route useful. Airport business-trip shares do not establish premium willingness to pay. Visitors may already have Uber installed; that advantage is discussed in the Danish authority's S11 p. 151.
- **P reachability:** hotel/venue booking guidance, staff demonstrations and local travel-search content. Proposed partner links must show actual coverage and terms. No named hotel partnership, commission, booking integration or partner willingness is assumed.
- **U ability to pay and needs:** party size, luggage, accessible-vehicle requirements, reimbursement, onward travel, language, payment method and preferred booking channel. Verify rather than equating visitors with high spend.
- **Boundary:** airport access, pick-up rules, permits, service geography and capacity are separate checks. Both local company product FAQs advertise pre-booking up to 30 days (PDFs in §1, p. 3); actual airport eligibility and booking fulfilment remain untested. Schiphol is not one of the named Amsterdam pilot districts. The Copenhagen airport volume does not prove Green SM airport service. Exclude airport-led acquisition until these points are verified. Do not guarantee flight tracking, luggage capacity or accessible transport.
- **Counter-argument:** weak repeat use after a short stay, acquisition commissions, incumbent familiarity and public-transport alternatives may undermine value. TCA's published Schiphol offer (NL TCA row, p. 1) already gives a concrete airport-price comparison; Green SM cannot claim a missing market merely because it is new.
- **Validation:** obtain permission for short visitor/partner discovery, distinguish in-city from airport requests, inspect allowed booking flow and matched competitor quotes. Reject S3 priority if most reachable demand lies beyond coverage or requires unverified airport access.

### Why not target all three?

P: use concentrated rather than simultaneous differentiated marketing (`targeting-strategies`, `w04-tutorial` s5). S1 needs consumer acquisition and post-trip follow-up; S2 adds procurement, account operations and consultative sales; S3 adds partners, traveller support and potentially airport permissions. A limited operating footprint cannot credibly support all promises at once. Broad coverage would also make attribution and capacity allocation harder to interpret. Secondary organic enquiries may still be served if eligible; that is not a separate paid targeting programme.

**Operating permission is a prerequisite, not a marketing benefit.** F: Denmark requires operator permission, a licensed dispatch office to sell taxi rides and a driver card (DK-15–DK-17); Green SM Denmark appears on the saved dispatch-office register (DK-18), which does not prove every proposed route, driver or vehicle is authorised. F: Dutch national guidance requires an operator licence and driver diploma/card (NL rows “Taxi operators need…” and “Drivers need…”, `netherlands-enterprise-agency-nd-taxi-operator-licence.pdf` p. 1 and `netherlands-enterprise-agency-nd-taxi-drivers-card.pdf` p. 1). Amsterdam rank/street-hail TTO requirements are separate from app bookings (`rijksoverheid-nd-extra-eisen-taxi-gemeenten.pdf` pp. 1–2). P: retain app-booked local trips as the proposed acquisition scope until permissions, current hours/geography, staffed availability and accessible-vehicle requirements are checked. The evidence of a company launch or local entity is not a substitute for that operational verification.

### Resource and evaluation implications, without invented totals

P: budget separately for local content/translation, limited media, any approved incentive liability, salesperson time, support capacity, CRM/analytics, service verification and contingency. Fleet investment is not silently counted as a marketing expense or assumed free. DKK applies to a Copenhagen model; EUR to Amsterdam; choose one period and currency only after country selection. ## 7. Hanoi fallback: a comparison, not another full plan

Hanoi is a fallback **only if the group rejects the European options on evidence or feasibility**, not an additional target market. VN V01 provides Hanoi's population, but the useful usage/attitude evidence comes mainly from multi-city or national samples: Rakuten V06–V11 covers five cities, not Hanoi alone; TGM V12–V14 is national; Q&Me V15–V16 selects weekly car users in four locations. None supplies an observed Hanoi persona.

I: a resident repeat-car-user/comfort proposition would be more grounded here than an untested European environmental premium. Rakuten reports comfort/cleanliness associations for Xanh SM (V10), while price matters in the national TGM survey (V14). P: test whether maintaining that experience supports repeat paid use against GrabCar and Be, rather than claiming cheapest service. V42 is one off-peak Hanoi trip with promotions; it is not a general price ranking. V29's four-wheel GMV estimates are disputed by Grab (V31) and must not be combined with overall app-usage shares.

The fallback's advantage is richer ride-hailing customer evidence and an established local service (E-002), not guaranteed profitability. The trade-off is loss of the group's European-entry focus and a different problem: defending experience and retention rather than introducing an unfamiliar entrant. Current Hanoi tariff, service capacity and amended legal position need checking; V35–V39 warn against using repealed Decree 10/2020 as current law. The India K1–K6 pack was also read for comparison, but no Delhi plan is developed: its national/Delhi evidence boundaries and early-service uncertainties do not authorise transplanting its segment findings to Copenhagen, Amsterdam or Hanoi.
