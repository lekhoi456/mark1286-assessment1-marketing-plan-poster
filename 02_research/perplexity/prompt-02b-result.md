# Bangladesh Assistive-Mobility Market Evidence Report

**Scope:** Evidence relevant to a proposed licensing and local-fitting arrangement for Shonaquip in Bangladesh. This is an evidence report only; it makes no recommendation. The strongest verified evidence concerns need, access gaps, existing production and disability law. Detailed programme-level distribution, payer, tariff and bilateral-trade data were not found in sufficiently authoritative and accessible sources.

## A. Need

### Cerebral palsy

A population-based surveillance study identified 726 children with cerebral palsy in rural Bangladesh between January 2015 and December 2016. It reported an observed prevalence of **3.4 per 1,000 children** (95% CI 3.2–3.7) and extrapolated an estimated **233,514 children nationally** (95% CI 219,778–254,118); **78.2%** of the identified children had never received rehabilitation [Khandaker et al., direct URL](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30394528/). The article was published online on 5 November 2018 and in the May 2019 journal issue; all figures above use 2015–2016 data. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30394528/)

The estimate should be described as an extrapolation from geographically defined rural surveillance, not as a complete national census. The authors also cautioned that delayed diagnosis and survival bias could produce under-identification [direct URL](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30394528/). [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30394528/)

### Device access

The Bangladesh Cerebral Palsy Register recorded 2,852 children between January 2015 and December 2019. Among 2,836 children with available rehabilitation information, **49.8% had never received rehabilitation**; among 2,090 children classified at GMFCS III–V, only **4.7%—98 children—had received any assistive device** [Al Imam et al., direct URL](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250640). The study was published on 3 May 2021; its figures cover 2015–2019. [journals.plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250640)

The study did not report wheelchairs, posture-support seating or standing devices as separate categories. Consequently, a wheelchair-specific coverage percentage for Bangladeshi children was **not found**.

### Broader child disability

Analysis of Bangladesh’s 2019 Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey found functional difficulties in **2.78% of children aged 2–4** and **8.27% of children aged 5–17** [direct URL](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hsr2.1023). The article was published on 26 December 2022 and used 2019 data; these are multi-domain functional-difficulty measures, not estimates specifically of physical disability or wheelchair need. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hsr2.1023)

A separate analysis of the same 2019 survey found 394 of 14,072 children aged 2–4—**2.8%**—had at least one functional difficulty [direct URL](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0300403). Published on 20 March 2024, it included seeing, hearing, walking, fine-motor, communication, learning, playing and behaviour domains and therefore cannot be treated as a physical-disability figure. [journals.plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0300403)

An official national figure specifically for **children with physical disabilities**, using a clearly stated childhood age range, was **not found**.

## B. Government provision

During Bangladesh’s 2022 examination by the UN Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, the government delegation stated that approximately **57,000 assistive devices were provided free of charge in 2021** [OHCHR, direct URL](https://www.ohchr.org/en/meeting-summaries/2022/08/experts-committee-rights-persons-disabilities-commend-bangladesh). The OHCHR summary was published on 25 August 2022. [ohchr](https://www.ohchr.org/en/meeting-summaries/2022/08/experts-committee-rights-persons-disabilities-commend-bangladesh)

The source does not identify:

- How many of the 57,000 products were wheelchairs.
- How many recipients were children.
- Whether products included customised seating or postural support.
- The allocation among Jatiyo Protibondhi Unnayan Foundation, Department of Social Services, disability service centres and other agencies.
- Assessment, prescription, fitting or follow-up procedures.

A current official programme table giving **scheme name, products, eligibility, annual quantity and whether each product is free** was **not found**. Therefore, no quantity should be attributed separately to JPUF, DSS or disability service centres from the evidence located.

[who](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549080)

### Medical devices

Bangladesh’s **Registration Guidelines for Medical Devices, 2015** use a risk-based Class A–D system. Class A manufacturers follow a declaration-of-conformity route, while devices in Classes B, C and D must be registered before first import or manufacture; an imported-device application must be made through a local authorised person or agent [official Gazette PDF](https://www.dpp.gov.bd/upload_file/gazettes/33970_33126.pdf). [dpp.gov](https://www.dpp.gov.bd/upload_file/gazettes/33970_33126.pdf)

The guideline also requests details of any local part-processing planned in Bangladesh. However, an explicit DGDA entry classifying manual wheelchairs, paediatric wheelchairs or postural-support seating as Class A, B, C or D was **not found**; therefore registration status for these specific products cannot be stated conclusively.

### Licensing payments

Bangladesh Bank’s foreign-exchange guidance states that Section 18 of the BIDA Act 2016 requires private-sector industrial enterprises to obtain BIDA approval for remittances of royalties and fees for technical knowledge, technical assistance and franchises. Authorised dealer banks must check the BIDA approval, tax payment and supporting documents before remittance [Bangladesh Bank, direct PDF](https://www.bb.org.bd/aboutus/regulationguideline/foreignexchange/feguidevol1/10.pdf). [bb.org](https://www.bb.org.bd/aboutus/regulationguideline/foreignexchange/feguidevol1/10.pdf)

A subsequent BIDA framework reported royalty and technical-fee ceilings of **6% of accumulated machinery import value before commercial operation** or **6% of previous-year sales excluding VAT after commercial operation**; the report also stated that technical-assistance fees up to US$10,000 per agreement could be remitted once annually [The Business Standard, direct URL](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/bida-eases-royalty-franchise-fee-transfer-abroad-221110). The source’s publication date is not displayed in the retrieved record; the data concern the 2021 BIDA circular. [tbsnews](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/bida-eases-royalty-franchise-fee-transfer-abroad-221110)

A separate statutory requirement to register the **licensing contract itself**, apart from BIDA/foreign-exchange endorsement and remittance approval, was **not found**.

## F. Law and policy

Section 16(1)(ঢ) of the **Rights and Protection of Persons with Disabilities Act 2013** establishes the right of a person with disability to receive support services and rehabilitation facilities to develop physical, mental and technical capacities and participate fully in society [official Bangladesh Laws URL](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1126.html). The Act is dated 8 October 2013. [bdlaws.minlaw.gov](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1126.html)

The Act recognises cerebral palsy separately within Bangladesh’s statutory disability framework and prohibits discriminatory denial of the rights listed in Section 16. It establishes national and local coordination structures and provides complaint and compensation mechanisms [direct URL](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1126.html). [bdlaws.minlaw.gov](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1126.html)

The **National Action Plan on Disability 2019** was adopted by the Cabinet in 2019. A 2023 policy analysis reports that it contains **18 action areas**, assigns functions to **35 ministries and departments**, and organises activities by objectives, performance indicators, timeframes, resources and responsible authorities [Sightsavers, direct PDF](https://campaigning.sightsavers.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Final-Somotar-Bangladesh-Policy-Analysis-Report.pdf). [campaigning.sightsavers](https://campaigning.sightsavers.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Final-Somotar-Bangladesh-Policy-Analysis-Report.pdf)

The plan includes community-based rehabilitation and fitting of artificial aids and appliances. A separate, current and formally adopted **national assistive-technology policy** or **national wheelchair policy** was **not found**.

## G. Payers

Evidence identifies a mixed system but does not quantify each payer’s national share:

- **Government:** Approximately 57,000 assistive devices were reportedly supplied free in 2021, but the value, product composition and recipients were not reported [OHCHR, direct URL](https://www.ohchr.org/en/meeting-summaries/2022/08/experts-committee-rights-persons-disabilities-commend-bangladesh). [ohchr](https://www.ohchr.org/en/meeting-summaries/2022/08/experts-committee-rights-persons-disabilities-commend-bangladesh)
- **NGOs:** NGO-run centres were the most common reported source of rehabilitation among children receiving services in the Bangladesh Cerebral Palsy Register—**45.1%, or 626 of 1,387 children with source information**, using 2015–2019 data [PLOS One, direct URL](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250640). [journals.plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250640)
- **International donors:** Motivation supported technical training at CRP, while ICRC supported CRP and rehabilitation services in Cox’s Bazar [Motivation PDF](http://motivation.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Motivation-Report-2022-WEB.pdf); [ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/bangladesh-first-ever-wheelchair-basketball-tournament-fosters-social-inclusion). [motivation.org](http://motivation.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Motivation-Report-2022-WEB.pdf)
- **Families:** The CP-register study identified direct service costs and indirect transport, food and accommodation costs as access barriers, but did not provide a mean or median household device expenditure [PLOS One, direct URL](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250640). [journals.plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250640)

A nationally representative figure for household **out-of-pocket spending on wheelchairs, seating or assistive products** was **not found**. The available evidence therefore cannot establish what percentage of devices is financed by government, NGOs, donors or families.

## H. Use environment

A reliable Bangladesh-specific study quantifying how rural road surfaces, monsoon flooding, doorway width, floor levels, sanitation access or housing density affect wheelchair use was **not found**.

The cerebral-palsy study identifies transport cost and service distance as rehabilitation-access barriers, but it does not measure wheelchair durability, terrain performance or in-home manoeuvrability [direct URL](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250640). [journals.plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250640)

Claims that particular wheel sizes, frames or seating systems are required for Bangladeshi conditions would therefore go beyond the verified evidence.
