A separate exact employee total for **Shonaquip (Pty) Ltd alone was not found**. No trainees, parents, programme participants or beneficiaries have been counted as employees here.

## D. Products

### Madiba timing

The exact first-design or launch year is contradictory. Design Indaba's article of 19 February 2025 says Madiba2Go is the latest version of an original buggy McDonald designed in **1994**, while Ashoka says the first locally produced battery-powered buggy provided the foundation for establishing Shonaquip in **1992**; the 2021 article states only that the original MadibaBuggy was developed “in the 1990s”.[^6][^7]

Accordingly, the safest fact-check is: **original Madiba/MadibaBuggy developed in the 1990s; exact launch date not found**. The date **1994** can be quoted only with attribution to Design Indaba, not presented as uncontested.

### Device differences

Shonaquip's Madiba is designed for children and young adults who cannot self-propel or sit independently and need more postural support than a conventional wheelchair or stroller. Its seating can be configured to support and align the body; individual fitting considers the child's body, function, lifestyle and environment, and follow-up modification is important as a child's body grows and changes.[^8][^9]

The original and later Madiba models use wide off-road wheels and sturdier frames for gravel, sand, mud, potholes and hills. The designs are intended to be maintainable and repairable where specialist workshops are unavailable; Madiba2Go adds modular adjustability, recline and easier transport/storage for carers.[^29][^7]

### Published prices

A current public retail price was **not found**. SSE's online catalogue displays **R0.00**, which functions as a quote/enquiry listing and must not be reported as the product's price. A TimesLIVE report dated 7 April 2014 stated that Madiba2Go sold for **R9,500**; this is a historical 2014 price only and is not evidence of today's price.[^30][^10]

## E. Delivery model

### Client pathway

1. **Identification and referral:** a child may enter via a government clinic/hospital, private therapist/client pathway, community outreach or a donor-supported project.[^9][^11]
2. **Assessment and prescription:** a trained seating practitioner assesses physical and cognitive needs, posture, function, daily activities, home terrain, transport and available support; remote assistance can be provided to a local therapist when the family is distant.[^7][^9]
3. **Selection and preparation:** the appropriate chair, cushion, seating system and accessories are selected and assembled.[^19]
4. **Individual fitting/customisation:** the practitioner and often a technician adjust supports, cushion and frame to achieve fit, postural support, mobility and function.[^19][^9]
5. **Training:** the user and carers receive training in safe use, transfers, components, mobility, maintenance and simple repairs.[^11][^19]
6. **Follow-up:** SSE says fitting is checked at roughly three-to-four-month reviews; its donation guide says formal reviews should occur at least once or twice yearly, with repairs and modifications as the child grows.[^9][^11]

### Who pays

Government health departments procure devices through tenders for public-sector provision; the 2021 article reported that government tenders purchased 60% of devices at that time. Private and medical-aid clients pay through the private route, while corporate sponsors, development/human-rights funders and other donors can finance devices for people on public waiting lists.[^7]

## J. Red flags and inconsistencies

### Financial pressure

The FY2024/25 report describes uncertainty, constrained budgets and delayed opportunities; a planned growth year became one of improved efficiency, reduced costs and new financial-resilience systems. Shonaquip's reserves were **R1.518m against a R4.1m target**, only 37%, which the report calls a **material shortfall**.

Uhambo Foundation reported **R17.956m revenue but an R868,000 deficit**, attributed to fuller direct cost allocation and old project budgets that had omitted the updated allocations. Uhambo reserves were **R2.525m versus a R3.5m target (72%)**; the Trust held **R206,000 versus a R408,000 target (50.5%)**.

Countervailing evidence is important: external auditors issued clear reports with no significant findings, reported no fraud, and confirmed that all three entities were solvent and liquid for the year ended 31 March 2025.

### Governance and data issues

- **Leadership terminology:** McDonald is “Founder & Executive Director” on the team page and “Founder and Managing Director” in another SSE article. No public resolution or appointment record was found to reconcile the titles.[^1][^25]
- **Founding/product chronology:** sources place the founding in 1992 but disagree on whether the formative buggy preceded that date or the original Madiba design dates to 1994.[^6][^7]
- **Headcount scope:** the 75-person figure is presented for SSE, not Shonaquip (Pty) Ltd separately; LinkedIn's 51–200 band is too broad to resolve the legal-entity count.[^5][^4]
- **Impact-report data quality:** the report says its figures came from multiple systems in transition and required substantial manual cleaning; it also warns that participant totals are not cumulative and datasets differ by indicator.
- **Export disclosure:** the latest report gives export revenue and some territories but no complete country-by-country sales, distributor list or export value by market.

### Negative-event search

Reliable public evidence of a Shonaquip product recall, material legal dispute or regulatory enforcement action was **not found**. That finding is limited by public-source availability and should not be converted into an affirmative claim that none has ever occurred.
