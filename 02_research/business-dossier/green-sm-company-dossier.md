### 5.2 Price evidence: list, cap and transaction are different

**Denmark has a saved tariff.** `green-sm-denmark-aps-ndb-green-sm-car.pdf`, p. 3, “Green Taxi Meter”, lists **Day (Min) / Night (Max)**: “Opening fee” **39 / 49**, “Next km onwards” **11 / 14**, “Price/min” **6.5 / 7.5** (DKK; literal table headings retained). It says “Prices last updated on 24 June 2026.” Separately, the same page says:

> “App prices, maximum (06-18) - Start rate: 94,58 DKK, Per km: 14.1 DKK, Per min: 7,8 DKK”
>
> “App prices, maximum (18-06) - Start rate: 105,52 DKK, Per km: 20,03 DKK, Per min: 8,8 DKK”

The holiday maximum additionally uses a **DKK/hour** time rate, not per minute. Do not combine meter and app schedules, treat maxima as actual prices, or call a calculated trip an observed fare. The article's June tariff update predates the July launch; preserve both dates.

**Vietnam:** `comet-vn-fares-news.pdf`, p. 4, Hanoi row gives **30,500 VND for the first 2 km**, then 14,700 / 13,800 / 11,900 in successive distance bands; p. 5 gives **34,400 VND for Premium's first 2 km**. These are listed examples on a mutable article, not a verified historical 2023 or September 2026 app basket. `comet-vn-car.pdf`, p. 2: “Prices booked through the app are determined via the pricing software” and “may include discounts (if any) and surcharge policies (if any)”.

**Netherlands and India:** the saved product-page text gives pricing conditions but no numeric tariff table in the inspected captures. Netherlands p. 3: “Final fares displayed in the Green SM App may vary depending on distance, time, applicable surcharges, tolls, promotions and local operating conditions.” India `comet-in-limo.pdf`, p. 2: “The actual trip cost depends on the actual distance, tolls (if any), and any additional fees (if any)”. This is **capture-level incompleteness, not proof that no public tariff exists**. India's INR8/km lead is not confirmed company pricing: BusinessLine p. 4 quotes, “Some figures currently circulating are speculative and have not been confirmed by Green SM”.

## 7. Strong support, disconfirming evidence and source dependence

1. **Service-control proposition has tangible inputs, but its result is unproved.** Fleet ownership, driver training, qualifications, a depot/charging route and booking/receipt mechanisms are explicitly described (§§4–5). None proves on-time performance, trust, five-star satisfaction or unit profitability. The distinction matters more than adding unsupported scale statistics.
2. **There is some independent customer support in Vietnam, not Europe.** `_staging/vietnam/rakuten-insight-2025-ride-hailing-app-landscape-vietnam.pdf`, p. 3: “Xanh SM is highly rated for driver services and hygiene, offering clean, new vehicles”; the same page says “Grab is the most often used brand” and associates it with “quick availability (26%) and a simple booking process (17%)”. Its 32% Xanh SM usage is not revenue market share.

   **Q&Me adds a separate, narrower car-user survey.** Reuse existing `q-and-me-nd`, [saved root PDF](../../04_references/q-and-me-nd-car-ride-hailing-usage-habits-2024.pdf), not a new company-manifest entry. 6. **Source independence is limited.** Root corporate launch releases, Vingroup copies, company pages and media reproductions can share one origin. Reuters adds reported interviews/analysis; Rakuten adds customer data with a restricted population. A count of PDFs is not a count of independent confirmations. Undated About pages retain old CEO text, source publication dates differ from events, and old news articles have new brand/product wording. Recheck decisive company facts before final approval.

## 8. Decision-ready boundaries and acquisition gaps

### Safe factual boundaries now

- A Vietnamese-origin mobility business with an eight-market footprint including a Dutch pilot; a single focus-market plan remains unapproved.
- Electric passenger transport is the common service thread. Rental, Platform, delivery, membership and business transport are **market-specific** capabilities, not a uniform offer across eight countries.

- Europe's useful factual distinction is managed service delivery and local operating standards, **not unverified category-first status or electricity alone**. No chosen position follows automatically.
- Mission statements can anchor proposals; measurable acquisition, repeat-use, service and financial objectives must remain explicitly proposed assumptions until approved. No numerical objective, budget or market score is adopted here.
