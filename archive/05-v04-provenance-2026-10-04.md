## Argument and customer evidence

The reading order is customer signal → tactical response → channel plan → bounded mechanism test. The priority ribbon follows S1: Copenhagen residents aged 25–44 who self-pay for repeat taxi trips (D-043; E-182–E-183). The 12-month sequence is retained, and no budget, objective, channel envelope or KPI target is changed.

E-024 leads the panel. The Competition and Consumer Authority's decision, table 5.12, PDF p.150, records stated reasons for the last platform-app choice; p.151 discloses n=79 and Region Hovedstaden. Lower price was selected by 57%, habitual app use by 22%, and shorter waiting and ease of use by 18% each. The source permits multiple responses. The visual names the Capital Region and platform-app-chooser scope and does not present the figures as all Copenhagen consumers, causal effects or willingness to pay.

E-023 remains separate national app-install context: the same authority decision, PDF p.194, reports published ranges among 1,005 recent Danish taxi users. One installed taxi app is shown as 40–50%; none as 30–40%. The ranges are not averaged or presented as Copenhagen data.

## 4Ps application

`marketing-mix-4ps` is an outside concept now registered in the course register, sourced to OpenStax (2023), *Principles of Marketing*, “The Marketing Mix and the 4Ps of Marketing”, saved PDF p.1. The source identifies product, price, place and promotion as the four decisions. This panel applies each to the Green SM plan: the electric taxi service (E-011); clearer meter/app-fare terms in response to the lower-price signal (E-015 and E-024); one local information-page-to-Green-SM-App booking route in response to habitual app choice (E-188 and E-024); and the plan's search/social channels. Wait/ease responses map to checking service area, capacity and support before making claims. The framework structures the recommendation; it does not validate the proposed mix or imply cheaper fares.

The local page is an information route, while the advertised booking instruction is in the Green SM App. No web checkout, booking conversion or causal uplift has been tested or claimed.

## First-party data / CDP readiness

`customer-data-platform` is an outside concept now registered in the course register, sourced to the CDP Institute, *What is a Customer Data Platform (CDP)?*, saved PDF p.1. Its definition describes a persistent, unified customer record accessible to other systems and a system accountable for identity and record structure. This source defines the concept; it does not show that Green SM operates such a system or has the data, interfaces or controls needed to do so.

The visual marks the path as a proposal and conditions use on data being available and consented: minimal first-party data → CRM/support review → a permissioned repeat reminder. Before treating the future state as CDP-ready, privacy, data quality, access and system readiness need checking. No current Copenhagen CRM integration, app-level customer-data access, CDP or new technology spend is claimed. The concept and data route are planning options, not delivered capability.

## Voucher mechanism

The Vietnam example is a documented mechanism, not a result: E-160/E-161 describe one-purchase and limited-validity membership terms; E-168 is a historical capped voucher package; E-170 describes optional repurchase after codes are used. Locators are the saved company pages and membership-launch article, PDF pp.1–3; the Green SM dossier §5.1 records the boundaries. None demonstrates retention lift, Danish availability or automatic renewal.

The Copenhagen capped 30-day, opt-in pack is expressly a proposal. It has no auto-renewal and only optional repurchase. Before any launch, app redemption, margin, service, legal terms, consent and support readiness must be checked. Any pilot must stay within the existing DKK12,000 reserve. The approved plan currently assigns that reserve to 400 × DKK30 first-ride vouchers, so changing its mechanic needs Section 6 and Section 7 owner acceptance. No additional spend is proposed.
