## Recommendation

Choose **A: compact age evidence beside the audience conditions**. The population chart supports the demographic choice; an equal-branch connection leads to the three approved conditions. This explains the selected audience in the recovered space rather than making population size the whole targeting argument. ## Meaningfully different approaches

| Dimension | A: evidence beside conditions | B: conditions first, evidence below |
|---|---|---|
| Main reading path | Demographic evidence → three conditions → accepted profile | Three pictogram conditions → compact central evidence → accepted profile |
| Segmentation visibility | One equal-branch bracket makes the common selection explicit | Three conditions are immediately prominent; their intersection is conveyed by the shared headline |
| Approximate chart layout envelope | 875 × 463 px; 54.9% of the old envelope | 875 × 389 px; 46.1% of the old envelope |
| Redrawing complexity | Four bars, one shared branch and three reused pictograms | Four bars and three reused pictograms; no shared branch |
| Maintenance | A single chart plus a single non-quantitative branch; no extra copy | A single chart; more care needed to maintain vertical clearances |
| Consequence | Readers can distinguish population context from the additional rider conditions | Readers see the proposed audience earlier; the supporting chart competes more closely with the row above |
| Main risk | A leader could be mistaken for a measured funnel if later stage counts/arrows were added | More compact vertical space makes future typography expansion harder |

**Simplest viable option:** B uses fewer connecting strokes. **Recommended option:** A makes the selected intersection clearer and gives the assessed segmentation story more useful structure. Both reuse existing illustration islands, so neither requires another image-generation call. Render/export commands take seconds locally; no time or cost claim is made for physical redrawing.
