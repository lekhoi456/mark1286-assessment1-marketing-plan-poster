## Option A — Rider benefits at the centre

A large rider-facing phone anchors the composition. Two illustrated satellites attach to the app: **Clear terms** contains area, hours and fare pictograms; **Local care** contains a trip-reference ticket and help headset. A small operating-model comparison hangs directly from the phone's base as its factual context. There are no stacked prose cards or directional booking steps.

**Strength:** makes the two planned rider benefits immediately legible and retains a named rival frame without allowing it to dominate. The phone is distinct from panel 2's central resident and demographic chart. A small taxi remains inside the phone, so it does not repeat panel 1's large vehicle illustration.

**Trade-off:** the named competitive frame is secondary. In a small car panel, both satellites and the two lower operator labels may need simplification. Their wording must not imply that Uber or Bolt lacks clarity or support.

## Option B — Green SM between named rivals

A much larger Green SM phone and electric-taxi illustration form one central offer. The two benefit accents connect directly to it and carry the **Proposed position** qualifier on the focal app. Smaller Uber and Bolt app symbols flank the offer. Their compact peripheral branches show only the known taxi-operator relationships. Green SM's owned fleet and employed-driver symbols sit at the base of its central illustration.

**Strength:** creates a recognisable comparison silhouette with Green SM visually dominant. Named rivals are present in the same composition, while the two planned benefits express the desired position. This is closer to the student's central-product/flanking-rivals reference as relayed by the parent.

**Trade-off:** the diagram can become an operating-model panel if the partner names or links grow. Keep these peripheral in later production. The central taxi also repeats an illustration type seen in panel 1; a cropped or smaller foreground taxi would help distinguish its role as the proposed rider offer. The enlargement communicates editorial focus, not performance, market share or measured consumer preference.

## Boundaries and source basis

The target remains Copenhagen residents aged **25–44** within the confirmed usable service area, who already take recurring local taxi trips and pay personally (D-032–D-033, D-043). The compact target line in these schematic alternatives is not a replacement for the parent's complete positioning statement.

The proposed benefit labels apply approved P1: clear terms and local care, with a route to resolution. They are planned emphasis, not observed service performance. No prices, service ratings, safety rankings or unverified rival weaknesses appear.

The factual comparison uses only the existing authority evidence: Green SM owns and operates its fleet and employs drivers (E-019, saved authority PDF p. 174); Uber-booked rides are operated by Drivr and Dantaxi, and Bolt-booked rides by Viggo, now branded Bolt, and Taxa 4x27 (E-184, saved PDF p. 119). These relationships do not prove better customer outcomes. The authority citation is adjacent to the model depiction in each option.

Module concepts remain `positioning`, `unique-value-proposition`, `positioning-strategies` and `positioning-statement`, as verified in the concept register. No external positioning method or fake metric chart is added. Panel 4 remains the home of brand identity and messaging.
