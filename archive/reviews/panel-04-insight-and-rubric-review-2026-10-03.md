# Panel 4 — customer insight, brief coverage and proposed improvement

Date: 3 October 2026. Status: DONE_WITH_CONCERNS. Read-only focused review of selected masters v01/v02, complete production v03 and the existing Copenhagen plan. No approved copy, image, strategy, shared control or accepted section was changed. Parent owns new evidence research and any adoption decision.

## Main conclusion

The current panel covers the brief's explicit logo, colour scheme and messaging requirements. It also distinguishes inherited corporate identity from a proposed local campaign promise. Its weaker point is the visible explanation of why that promise fits the target audience. The phrase about trip control and fair treatment is an approved targeting hypothesis, not a finding about Copenhagen residents or all riders aged 25–44.

The local promise is partly informed by Danish taxi-market evidence and the selected positioning. It has not been validated by research with the exact target segment. The inspected sources do not establish an EU-wide consumer insight. Global logo and palette retention follows the real company's identity decision; these assets do not need to be reinvented from local consumer preferences.

Confidence: **high** on requirement coverage and the limits of the inspected evidence; **moderate** on the proposed creative improvement; **unknown** on the target audience's actual response and the eventual mark.

## Evidence to promise: what is supported

The registered authority PDF was inspected at pp. 150–151 and 194. Page 150 paragraph 726 supplies the Capital Region scope; page 151 states n = 79 and multiple responses. The evidence log retains the exact claims and limitations.

| Existing evidence | Defensible use in branding | Inference it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| E-023: national taxi-user sample n = 1,005; installed-app counts reported as ranges | Recognisable, consistent branding is a reasonable response to a market where many respondents have only one installed taxi app | A Copenhagen-specific habit rate, age-specific behaviour, automatic loyalty or proven switching resistance |
| E-024: Capital Region app-choice subsample n = 79; published reasons include price 57%, habit 22%, waiting 18% and ease of use 18% | Acknowledge price and familiar/easy app use when designing a clear, repeatable message | Price sensitivity does not prove demand for transparency; habitual app use does not prove demand for superior support or Green SM's ability to provide it |
| E-187: the same small subsample; 11% cited reassurance/safety from driver information, trip tracking and status sharing | Some app users connect visible trip information with reassurance; this can inform an intended trip-control appeal | All target riders value this; fare clarity or fair treatment was measured; willingness to switch/pay; Green SM is safer or more helpful than rivals |

The integrated plan's §6 explicitly calls the trip-control emotional link a **hypothesis to test**. Its §14 identifies the unresolved question of whether S1 values clarity and help enough to switch. Its earlier campaign line is historical planning material: the later selected tagline **Clear terms. Local care.** controls. Do not restore old wording automatically.

“Local care” remains a selected proposed promise supported by the plan's intended delivery arrangements. These three consumer-evidence entries do not directly validate demand for local complaint support or fair treatment. Current source-backed operational details in section 3 do not prove customer preference or delivered service quality.

## Recommended redrawable infographic, proposal only

Keep the global identity → Copenhagen promise relationship and the authentic logo. Compact the two palette swatches rather than giving them such large areas. Use the freed space for a small, bounded market cue that feeds the proposed promise. The label on this connection should make a **chosen response** intelligible, rather than implying that the evidence proves every word of the slogan.

On the local side, retain the dominant yellow tagline ribbon. Place the market cue above it, with the geography/sample limit beside the cue. Keep the audience description visibly within the proposed targeting/promise region. Beneath the ribbon, show one simple campaign example using the same logo, palette and exact tagline; two small pen-outline touchpoints can demonstrate the same expression across a campaign ad and an app message. Treat these as illustrative applications, not existing or executed company material. Preserve Danish-first/English-second as the selected localisation instruction; an English working mock-up would not establish completed Danish copy.

This creates three visible decisions: **retain corporate identity; choose a local emotional promise informed by bounded evidence; apply the identity/message consistently**. It addresses C2's creative alignment more directly and gives C7's symbols a clear explanatory role.

Do not repeat section 3's own fleet, employed drivers, training, standards, customer-service procedures or rival operating models. Section 3 explains the offer and proposed delivery; section 4 should explain and demonstrate its brand expression. Do not add cultural stereotypes, colour psychology, an EU-wide preference claim, a “safer/better support” comparison or a price-leadership promise.
