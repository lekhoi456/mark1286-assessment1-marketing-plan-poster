# Section 5 candidate v03 — provenance and rationale

Status: prepared for parent review under D-080. This is a candidate, not group-approved copy or an accepted panel image.

## Argument and scope

The visual moves from the strongest local customer-choice signal to a proposed response, the channel allocation, and a bounded voucher test. The priority ribbon follows S1: Copenhagen residents aged 25–44 who self-pay for repeat taxi trips (D-043; E-182–E-183). The 12-month sequence is retained. No objective, KPI, total budget, channel envelope or target is changed.

E-024 supports the lead insight: the Competition and Consumer Authority's decision, table 5.12, PDF p.150, reports stated reasons for the last platform-app choice; p.151 discloses n=79 and Region Hovedstaden. Lower price was selected by 57%, habitual app use by 22%, and shorter wait and ease of use by 18% each. The source describes multiple responses. The caption therefore keeps the Capital Region and platform-app-chooser scope, and labels the figures regional multi-response rather than Copenhagen-wide preferences, willingness to pay or causal effects.

E-023 is shown as separate national context: the same authority decision, PDF p.194, reports published ranges for installed taxi apps among 1,005 recent taxi users in Denmark. The display preserves 40–50% for one app and 30–40% for none. These ranges are not averaged or recast as Copenhagen data.

The three response mappings are recommendations, not measured effects: the price signal leads to clearer fare terms; habitual choice leads to one proposed search/social → local information page → app-booking route; wait/ease responses lead to service-capacity and support checks before claims. E-188 (Green SM Denmark product page, PDF pp.1–2) supports that booking is instructed through the Green SM App and that the web page is informational. It does not establish tested web checkout, local availability, capacity or campaign conversion.

## Voucher mechanism and module scope

The visual labels Vietnam as an example and states that capped vouchers with limited validity have no evidenced retention lift. This boundary comes from E-160 and E-161 (Vietnam membership page, PDF pp.1–2), E-168 (historical student voucher package, PDF p.1) and E-170 (optional repurchase after promotional codes are used, PDF p.3), also summarised in the Green SM dossier §5.1. These records document mechanisms and terms, not business impact, current Danish availability or automatic renewal.

The Copenhagen 30-day opt-in pack is expressly a proposal. It has no auto-renewal and only optional repurchase; any test must first check app redemption capability, margin, service, legal terms, consent and support readiness. The visible cap keeps it within the existing DKK12,000 incentive reserve. The integrated plan currently assigns that reserve to 400 × DKK30 first-ride vouchers, so changing its mechanics requires Section 6 and Section 7 owner acceptance. No additional spend is proposed.

Register check: `omnichannel` and `consumer-insights-and-big-data` are present in the course register. The panel uses the customer-insight component of the latter; n=79 is not described as Big Data. It uses omnichannel only to recommend a consistent proposed path across touchpoints, not to claim connected systems. Marketing Mix/4Ps and CDP/customer data platform have no course-register entries. They are not named as module concepts, no outside concept is introduced, and no existing Copenhagen CDP/CRM integration or data capability is claimed. The registered subscription-bundling concept is not applied; there is no recurring billing or automatic-renewal claim.
