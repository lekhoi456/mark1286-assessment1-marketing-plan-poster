### C1 Company Background and Country Context: 6.5 / 10
Rubric: "Clearly and neatly presents the company's background, country of origin, and core products/services. Demonstrates a clear understanding of context."

- **Evidenced:** Cell 1 is neat and compact. It shows "Vingroup-backed", "Founded March 2023", "Owned fleet (590 registered) · Employed drivers", eight country flags with "Vietnam 14/04/2023", "Denmark 30/07/2026" and "Netherlands pilot", and the service line "Green SM Taxi: app-booked electric taxi rides in Copenhagen". A VinFast-badged car and a phone-to-car flow make the operating model clear.
- **Deductions:**
  - Country of origin appears only as a small house icon beside "Vietnam". No words say "Vietnamese company, headquartered in …".
  - "590 registered" does not say what is registered or where (Denmark? Copenhagen? group-wide?).
  - Core products and services are reduced to one service. A reader cannot tell whether the wider business offers anything else.
  - Country context for Denmark/Copenhagen (market size, regulation, EV/taxi landscape, why Copenhagen) is missing from this cell. Some context does appear in Cell 2 (population) and Cell 3 (competitors), so the deduction is partial.
  - The heading promises "Electric Taxi **Launch**", but Cell 1 dates the Danish launch at 30/07/2026 and Cell 7 runs the plan "Nov 2026–Oct 2027". The plan's status (entry or post-launch growth) is therefore unclear.

### C4 Budget & Resource Allocation: 11 / 15
Rubric: "Presents a visual budget (e.g., pie chart or table) that is well-organized. Demonstrates realistic allocation of resources and feasible budgeting."

- **Evidenced (Cell 7):**
  - "Proposed DKK600,000 · Nov 2026–Oct 2027 · excluding VAT", shown as a labelled horizontal bar chart.
  - The lines add up: Team 276,000 / 46%; Search 108,000 / 18%; Social 90,000 / 15%; Screens 43,195 / 7.2%; Tools 30,000 / 5%; Vouchers 12,000 / 2% ("400 riders"); Contingency 40,805 / 6.8%. The sum is DKK600,000 and 100%.
  - Team hours are built up: 60 + 120 + 160 + 120 = 460 h × DKK600/h = 276,000.
  - Stage gates: "M4 review DKK177,000 → M6 review DKK265,000 → Held at M6, Remaining DKK335,000", with "M6: 265,000 + 335,000 = 600,000".
  - Scope is stated: "Outside budget: app build, cars, drivers, charging / frontline support".
  - This is the strongest cell on the poster.
- **Deductions:**
  - **The unit check flatters the economics.** "DKK165 CAC repaid in about 2.4 rides at an assumed DKK68 contribution per ride" uses a CAC equal to (Search 108,000 + Social 90,000) / 1,200 riders, which is media only. Fully loaded, the cost is DKK600,000 / 1,200 = DKK500 per first paid rider. On Cell 8's own targets, 2,500 paid rides / 1,200 riders ≈ 2.1 rides per rider, which is below the 2.4 rides needed even for the media-only payback. Cell 10's caveat "first-year break-even not required" helps, but the poster should show the full figure, not just the favourable one.
  - Cell 9 says "Unpriced dispatch/chatbot/subscription: cost separately". The innovation pilots that carry C6 are therefore outside the budget.
  - "Screens" (DKK43,195) is not defined. The DKK600/h rate and the DKK68 contribution are labelled as assumptions, which is honest, but no basis is shown.
  - 460 hours over 12 months (about 9 h/week) for campaigns, creative/localisation, CRM and checks is lean, and its feasibility is not argued.

## 3. Strengths (max 5)

1. **The budget is visual, reconciled and gated (C4, Cell 7).** Seven lines sum exactly to DKK600,000 and 100%, team cost is built from hours × rate (460 h × DKK600), there is a 6.8% contingency, the period and VAT treatment are stated, and the M4/M6 gates hold DKK335,000 back. This meets "visual budget … well-organized" and "feasible budgeting".
2. **KPIs are quantified, attributed and staged (C5, Cell 8).** Five 12-month targets carry footnoted attribution rules, have M4/M6 interim thresholds, are measured with a baseline survey ("n=400 per wave"), and end in a "Renew · Revise · Stop" rule. This "shows understanding of how to evaluate marketing success".
3. **All three segmentation bases appear, with priorities stated (C2, Cell 2).** Demographic (official-style age data whose 40.2% figure checks), behavioural ("Existing taxi users", "Recurring local trips") and psychographic ("Price-conscious", "Values trip control") bases are present. Segments are prioritised ("Later: visitors · business accounts").
4. **Digital execution is localised (C3, Cells 4–5).** The Danish search ad ("bestil taxa … Book i appen."), "Danish first · English second" and the funnel from Copenhagen page to app to ride are concrete, relevant examples.
5. **Structure follows the brief and the plan is honest about uncertainty (C7 / brief).** All eleven required elements appear in brief order with complete student/business details, and the poster labels its uncertainty openly ("All targets and pilots are group proposals"; "PILOTS · Local performance untested").

## 4. Weaknesses (max 7)

1. **Text is too small and too dense to read at poster distance (C7, all cells; worst in Cells 5 and 8).** Body ≈11–14 pt at A0 and footnotes smaller. Telegraphic notation fails "easy to understand" and "well-organized" for a viewer without the presenter.
2. **Positioning ignores the poster's own customer insight, and the brand message is generic (C2, Cells 2–5).** "Lower price 57%" and "Price-conscious" sit beside "Similar fares" and an edge of "Direct control → one service standard". There is no formal positioning statement or customer-framed UVP, and "Go Green For a Green Future." does not differentiate when "Electric options" are "Shared".
3. **Digital and sales tactics lack named channels and examples (C3, Cells 5–6).** No social platform, content example or SEO plan is given, the only sales channel is the app, and B2B/partnership sales are deferred. This misses "relevant examples" and the brief's "social media, content marketing, SEO".
4. **The payback figure flatters the economics (C4, Cells 7–8).** DKK165 CAC counts search and social only (DKK198,000 / 1,200). The fully loaded cost is DKK500 per first paid rider, and the poster's own 2,500 rides / 1,200 riders ≈ 2.1 rides falls short of the 2.4 rides needed. The innovation pilots are "Unpriced".
5. **Key data cannot be traced to a source (brief: "Cite all your sources in the poster").** No front-of-poster citations appear. The figures "Denmark · n=1,005", "40–50% one app", "Capital Region · n=79 · ±11 pp", "57%" and "22%" cannot be traced to any of the 21 back-of-chart references, and neither can "Similar fares" or "Taxa 4x27". With n=79 and ±11 pp, the 57% cannot carry the weight placed on it. The list relies mainly on company and press pages: the only scholarly item is an arXiv preprint, and nothing comes from the module reading list.
6. **Creativity is broad but generic (C6, Cell 9).** The "neuro cue test" is an A/B test on CTR and recall, AI dispatch is operational, and no Copenhagen campaign idea is shown.
7. **Brief compliance gaps (brief; C7 "Evident effort from all group members"; C1).** Member contributions are not indicated, the canvas is A0 against "A1 Paper Chart", and the "Launch" title conflicts with "Denmark 30/07/2026" and a "Nov 2026–Oct 2027" plan.
