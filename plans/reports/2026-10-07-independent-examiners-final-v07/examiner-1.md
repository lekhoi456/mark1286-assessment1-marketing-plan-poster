## 2. Per-criterion justification

### C1. Company Background and Country Context: 7.0 / 10 (70%)
**Strengths**
- The essentials are clear and neat: "Vingroup-backed", "Founded March 2023", "Owned fleet (590 registered) · Employed drivers", "App-booked electric taxi rides · Denmark = 1st EU market".
- Country of origin is marked with a home icon ("Vietnam 14/04/2023"). An eight-flag footprint (Vietnam, Laos, Indonesia, Philippines, India, Kazakhstan, Denmark "30/07/2026", Netherlands "pilot") shows international scope at a glance.
- The service model is drawn as a phone → VinFast car flow, which shows the product quickly.

**Limits**
- The Danish country context is spread across Cells 2–3 (population, competitors). Cell 1 never says *why Denmark/Copenhagen*: no market size, regulation or EV-readiness rationale.
- "590 registered" has no scope (Denmark only, or group-wide?) and no date.
- The core offer is one line. The group's scale in its home market (fleet, rides, cities) is not given, so the reader cannot judge the company's capability to enter.

### C2. Target Market, Segmentation & Branding: 10.5 / 15 (70%)
**Strengths**
- The demographic sizing is sound and to scale: "Focus: Copenhagen residents aged 25–44", "269,277", "40.2% of city", "City population (all ages): 670,389", dated "Copenhagen · 1 Jul 2026 · 18+".
- All three bases are present. Behavioural: "Self-paying", "Existing taxi users", "Recurring local trips". Psychographic: "Price-conscious · 57%", "Values trip control", "Green by habit · 41%".
- Segments are prioritised and sequenced: "1 Focus: 25–44 · M1+", "3 Business accounts · M4+", "4 Visitors + airport · M6+".
- Positioning is clear and competitor-aware: "Electric rides. One managed experience." "For green, fair-fare riders: 100% electric · one company runs app, car + driver". A points-of-parity/points-of-difference logic ("Parity: app booking · electric options · Dantaxi/Drivr fares"; "Edge: Direct control → one service standard") sits beside a competitor map ("Uber → Drivr · Dantaxi (part to be sold)", "Bolt → Viggo/Bolt · Taxa 4x27").
- The identity specification is complete: logo, "Green SM = Green and Smart Mobility", "Go Green For a Green Future.", "New Cyan #28bdbf Pantone 319 C", "New Yellow #e3bb42", "Xanh Display 2.0", "Modern Liquid Glass UI/UX". It is localised: "Danish first: København, mød Green SM".

**Limits**
- "2 All adults · M1+" is not a segment. It contains Segment 1, so the four-segment logic is weakened.
- Segments 3 and 4 are not sized or profiled.
- The psychographic data are thin. "Price-conscious · 57%" appears to re-use the "Lower price 57%" figure from a "Capital Region · n=79 · ±11 pp" subsample and generalises it to the 25–44 focus group.
- Branding is not tied to the target. The group's own data put price (57%) ahead of green habit (41%), yet the brand message leads with "Go Green For a Green Future." and fares are at "Parity". The "Edge" is an operating feature, not a customer benefit.
- The brief asks for "a clear positioning statement"; the poster offers a tagline plus fragments.
- The identity is the parent brand's existing one. The only new creative element is the campaign line "Copenhagen, meet Green SM".

### C3. Digital Marketing & Sales Strategies: 13.5 / 20 (67.5%)
**Strengths**
- The digital tactics are practical and localised. Each channel is mapped to a KPI: "Search: bestil taxa → installs", "Social: Instagram · Facebook → CTR", "City screens + video → awareness", "Content: Copenhagen page → visits", "CRM: reminders · referral → repeat".
- There are real executional examples: the Danish search/landing copy "Elektrisk taxa. Tjek prisen. Book i appen.", and an omnichannel route "page → app → ride" with "Same fares / offers / help".
- A testable promotion is included: "DKK15 vs DKK30 First paid ride · 1 per rider · ≤DKK30", phased "M1–3 Test / M4 / M6 gates / M7–12 Scale if OK".
- Insight drives the channel choice: "Denmark · n=1,005 · 70–90% · 0–1 taxi apps", "Lower price 57%", "Usual app 22%".
- The sales funnel is clear: "Discover → First paid ride → Experience → Feedback → Repeat", with techniques: "Book now or pre-book 30 days", "First-ride offer DKK15/30", "Invite unpaid reviews", "Reminders · referral credit", "Commuter pass test".

**Limits**
- The sales process for the B2B revenue stream is missing. "Channels: app · B2B via LinkedIn (M4+) · partners (M6+)" is all that supports "≥100 business accounts by M12", "B2B sales 2.0M / 7%" and "B2B sales 2 FTE". The handbook asks for "lead generation, prospecting, and conversion"; there are no target account types, no outreach, no demo/trial, no contract or account management, and no named sales technique.
- "partners (M6+)" are unnamed.
- "4 Visitors + airport · M6+" has no tactic.
- The 4Ps are one-word entries ("Electric + service", "Clear fare + trial", "Page → app", "Channel plan →"), and price strategy beyond the trial offer is absent.
- Influencer marketing (a taught topic) and channels likely to reach 25–44s beyond Instagram/Facebook are not considered.

### C4. Budget & Resource Allocation: 11.5 / 15 (77%)
**Strengths**
- The visual budget is well organised: a bar-chart table "Proposed DKK30.0M", "Nov 2026–Oct 2027 · excluding VAT", with eight lines ("Search + app 7.0M / 23%" … "Contingency 2.0M / 7%"). The lines sum exactly to 30.0M and 100%, and the bar lengths are proportional.
- The method and assumption are transparent: "Objective-and-task · 6% of run-rate revenue*", "*Run-rate, assumed: 450 cars × 15 trips/day × DKK200".
- The governance is strong: "Budget reviews · cumulative caps M4 review DKK7.5M → M6 review DKK13.5M", "Held at M6 DKK16.5M" (13.5 + 16.5 = 30.0). Scope is explicit: "Outside budget: app build, cars, drivers, charging / frontline support". A people line is included ("Campaigns 3 FTE", "B2B sales 2 FTE").

**Limits**
- "Objective-and-task" and "6% of run-rate revenue" are different budgeting methods, and the label mixes them.
- The 6% is computed on the Month-12 annualised run-rate, not Year-1 revenue during ramp-up. The real Year-1 marketing-to-revenue ratio will therefore be well above 6%, and this is not acknowledged.
- The creativity pilots are explicitly unfunded ("Unpriced pilots: cost separately"), which undermines feasibility.
- The FTEs are not mapped to budget lines.
- "Incentives 4.6M" barely covers 150,000 × ≤DKK30 (= DKK4.5M), leaving about DKK0.1M for "referral credit" and the "Commuter pass test" in the worst case.
- Realism rests on unverified inputs (DKK200 average fare, 15 trips/car/day).

The "Edge: Direct control → one service standard" is an operating feature, and there is no formal positioning statement.
3. **No member contribution is shown**, despite the brief's instruction and the rubric's "Evident effort from all group members".
4. **The text is dense, small and in shorthand.** Many lines are about 2.5 mm cap height at A1. Abbreviations such as "Held at M6" and "†Full 90-day cohorts" need narration to be understood, and much of the canvas is decorative.
5. **The innovation is unfunded and underdeveloped.** "Unpriced pilots: cost separately" leaves the AI, bot, neuro and subscription ideas outside the DKK30.0M plan. Each is a few fragments with no threshold.
6. **Several figures are unexplained or untraceable.** These are "70–90%" (why a range?), "Uber + Dantaxi 40–50%" (of what?), and "590 registered" versus "450 cars". With no front-side citations, the marker cannot link any statistic to the reference list.

### 7c. Inconsistencies and tensions found
1. **Fleet: 590 vs 450.** "Owned fleet (590 registered)" (Cell 1) against "450 cars" (Cell 7 run-rate). The 140-car (24%) gap is unexplained. It may be an active-fleet or utilisation assumption, but the poster does not say so.
2. **Budget rounding.** 6% × (2,463,750 × DKK200 = DKK492.75M) = DKK29.57M, not "DKK30.0M" (which is 6.09%). The headline uses the rounded 2.5M trips. The difference is minor, but "30.0" implies precision.
3. **One figure, two meanings.** "57%" appears as "Lower price 57%" (a Capital Region subsample, n=79, ±11 pp) in Cell 5 and as "Price-conscious · 57%" in the 25–44 "Rider profile" in Cell 2. It is presented as a trait of the focus segment when it comes from a different, small base.
4. **Incentives headroom.** At ≤DKK30 per rider, 150,000 riders cost up to DKK4.5M against "Incentives 4.6M". That leaves about DKK0.1M for "referral credit" and the "Commuter pass test" if the DKK30 arm wins.
5. **CAC numerator label.** The formula uses "(search + social)", but the budget line is "Search + app". It is unclear whether app-store spend is in the CAC.
6. **Budget base.** "6% of run-rate revenue" uses M12 annualised revenue, so the actual Year-1 spend-to-revenue ratio is materially higher than the 6% shown in Cells 7 and 10.
7. **Unfunded pilots.** "Unpriced pilots: cost separately" (Cell 9) means the plan is not fully costed, which conflicts with the "Proposed DKK30.0M" as a complete plan.
8. **Missing KPI target.** "trips/car" is a review item ("M12 review: awareness · riders · trips/car · repeat · CAC") with no target in Cell 8. It is implied only by the "15 trips/day" footnote.
9. **Rider target realism.** 150,000 first paid riders = 55.7% of the 269,277 focus group, or 22.4% of the 670,389 city population. This is not arithmetically inconsistent, since Segments 2–4 add volume, but the poster does not show the split.
10. **Launch timing.** The Denmark service started "30/07/2026", while the plan runs "Nov 2026–Oct 2027" under the title "…Electric Taxi Launch". The plan is a post-launch scale-up, not a launch plan, and the two titles differ.
11. **Unexplained ranges.** "70–90%" and "40–50%" are ranges without explanation, possibly redacted bands from a competition-authority decision. The reader cannot tell what population or measure they describe.
12. **Compliance (not marked).** The pack describes the render as "A0 landscape", but the brief specifies "One Standard Size A1 Paper Chart". The group should confirm the submitted size.
