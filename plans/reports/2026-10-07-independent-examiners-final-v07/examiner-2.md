## 2. Per-criterion justification

### C1. Company Background and Country Context: 7.0 / 10
**Strengths.** The cell is specific and neat. It shows the ownership ("Vingroup-backed"), the founding date ("Founded March 2023"), the operating model ("Owned fleet (590 registered) · Employed drivers"), the home market ("Vietnam 14/04/2023" with a home icon), the market footprint (eight flags, including "Denmark 30/07/2026" and "Netherlands pilot"), and the service and entry logic ("App-booked electric taxi rides · Denmark = 1st EU market"). The country of origin, the core service and the target country all appear.
**Limits.**
- The Danish country context is thin in this cell. Nothing explains why Denmark or Copenhagen, and nothing covers taxi regulation, licensing or market size. Some context appears elsewhere (cells 2, 3 and 5), but the rubric asks for "a clear understanding of context", and this cell describes the company only.
- "590 registered" has no scope: Denmark, Copenhagen or group-wide? It also differs from the budget's "450 cars" with no explanation (see section 7).
- "Netherlands pilot" has no date, so a reader cannot tell whether "Denmark = 1st EU market" means first in time or first full launch.
- The products and services are a single line. The rubric asks for "core products/services", and only one service is described.

### C2. Target Market, Segmentation & Branding: 10.5 / 15
(Cells 2, 3 and 4 are taken together because the rubric groups target market, segmentation and branding, and positioning belongs with them.)
**Strengths.**
- *Demographic:* a bar chart of real age bands, "18–24 72,396 / 25–44 269,277 / 45–64 143,668 / 65+ 74,728", with "40.2% of city" and "City population (all ages): 670,389". The arithmetic is correct: 269,277 / 670,389 = 40.2%.
- *Behavioural:* "Self-paying", "Existing taxi users", "Recurring local trips".
- *Psychographic:* "Price-conscious · 57%", "Values trip control", "Green by habit · 41%".
- Segments are sequenced over time: "1 Focus: 25–44 · M1+ / 2 All adults · M1+ / 3 Business accounts · M4+ / 4 Visitors + airport · M6+".
- *Positioning:* a clear headline, "Electric rides. One managed experience.", a target-and-benefit line, "For green, fair-fare riders: 100% electric · one company runs app, car + driver", and a points-of-parity / point-of-difference logic: "Parity: app booking · electric options · Dantaxi/Drivr fares" against "Edge: Direct control → one service standard". Competitors are named.
- *Branding:* the logo; colours specified to production level ("New Cyan #28bdbf Pantone 319 C", "New Yellow #e3bb42"); a typeface ("Xanh Display 2.0"); the UI style ("Modern Liquid Glass UI/UX · Layered · soft · clear"); a Danish-first campaign line ("København, mød Green SM").

**Limits.**
- **The segmentation logic is weak.** "2 All adults · M1+" is not a segment. It contains segment 1 and starts in the same month ("M1+"). So the "Focus" is never actually prioritised over the mass market, and no criteria (size, accessibility, fit) explain why 25–44 was chosen.
- The psychographic figures are not shown to apply to the focus segment. The "57%" in "Price-conscious · 57%" is the same figure as "Lower price 57%" in cell 5, which is labelled "Capital Region · n=79 · ±11 pp". That is a small regional subsample, not Copenhagen residents aged 25–44, and it is relabelled from a stated reason ("Lower price") to a trait ("Price-conscious"). "Values trip control" has no figure.
- **The edge is an operating model, not a rider benefit.** "Direct control → one service standard" says what the company does, not what the rider gets or why that rider would switch from "Drivr · Dantaxi" when the fares are at parity.
- **The brand messaging does not fit the positioning.** The taglines "Go Green / For a Green Future." lead with the green attribute, but cell 3 lists "electric options" under **Parity**. The messaging sells what the poster itself calls non-differentiating.
- The identity is the parent brand's existing identity, adopted as it is. Apart from the Danish campaign line, no case is made for why it suits Copenhagen riders aged 25–44, which is what "aligned with the target audience" asks.
- The competitor shorthand cannot be read on its own: "Partner operators · Uber + Dantaxi 40–50%" (40–50% of what?), "Uber → Drivr · Dantaxi (part to be sold)", "Bolt → Viggo/Bolt · Taxa 4x27".

### C3. Digital Marketing & Sales Strategies: 13.5 / 20
**Strengths.**
- Digital is practical and localised. The omnichannel path is "Omnichannel: page → app → ride". There is a Danish search keyword, "bestil taxa", and Danish landing copy: "Elektrisk taxa / Tjek prisen. / Book i appen."
- The channel-to-KPI map is a good discipline: "Search: bestil taxa → installs / Social: Instagram · Facebook → CTR / City screens + video → awareness / Content: Copenhagen page → visits / CRM: reminders · referral → repeat".
- The launch is phased and tested: "LAUNCH CAMPAIGN Copenhagen, meet Green SM · city screens · video · social · search", "DKK15 vs DKK30 First paid ride · 1 per rider · ≤DKK30", "M1–3 Test / M4 / M6 gates / M7–12 Scale if OK".
- The 4Ps sit alongside: "Product Electric + service / Price Clear fare + trial / Place Page → app / Promotion Channel plan".
- Sales is laid out as a five-stage funnel with a loop back: "Discover → First paid ride → Experience → Feedback → Repeat". The techniques are named: "Book now or pre-book 30 days", "First-ride offer DKK15/30", "Invite unpaid reviews", "Reminders · referral credit", "Commuter pass test".

**Limits.**
- **There is no B2B or partner sales process.** The whole B2B channel is "B2B via LinkedIn (M4+) · partners (M6+)". The brief asks for "lead generation, prospecting, and conversion strategies". None is shown for business accounts, even though the budget funds "B2B sales 2.0M / 7%" and "2 FTE", and the KPI is "≥100 business accounts by M12". The partners are not named.
- Segment 4, "Visitors + airport · M6+", has no channel, tactic or partner.
- Social is limited to "Instagram · Facebook" for 25–44-year-olds. There is no influencer, short-video or advocacy tactic (the module covers influencer marketing in week 8), and no example content beyond the single landing page.
- The SEO and content tactics amount to one keyword and one page.
- The survey insights are not tied to the tactics. "70–90% 0–1 taxi apps" and "Usual app 22%" point to a switching barrier, but no tactic addresses switching.
- There are no conversion-rate assumptions linking spend to the 150,000 riders. Install-to-ride or click-to-install rates would have done this.

### C4. Budget & Resource Allocation: 11.0 / 15
**Strengths.**
- The budget is a clear, well-organised visual: horizontal bars with values and shares, "Proposed DKK30.0M · Nov 2026–Oct 2027 · excluding VAT".
- The lines reconcile exactly: 7.0 + 5.0 + 5.0 + 4.6 + 3.4 + 2.0 + 1.0 + 2.0 = 30.0, and the shares round to 100%.
- The basis is disclosed: "*Run-rate, assumed: 450 cars × 15 trips/day × DKK200".
- The cumulative caps reconcile: "M4 review DKK7.5M → M6 review DKK13.5M", "Held at M6 DKK16.5M", and 13.5 + 16.5 = 30.0.
- There is a 7% contingency, the people are shown ("Campaigns 3 FTE", "B2B sales 2 FTE"), and exclusions are explicit: "Outside budget: app build, cars, drivers, charging / frontline support".
- The budget agrees with the KPIs. The media lines (search + social) are 12.0M / 30.0M = 40% of spend. 40% of the M4 cap (7.5M) is DKK3.0M, which equals 25,000 riders × DKK120, the M4 CAC gate. At M12, 12.0M / 150,000 = DKK80, which is ≤ DKK100.

**Limits.**
- **The method label contradicts itself.** "Objective-and-task · 6% of run-rate revenue" names two different methods. The DKK30M total comes from the percentage-of-revenue rule, not from costed tasks.
- **The 6% is taken on an end-state run-rate, not year-one revenue.** 450 × 15 × 365 × DKK200 ≈ DKK493M, and 6% of that is DKK29.6M (DKK30.0M is 6.1%). Trips ramp from "≥2,500 paid trips a day" at M6 to about 6,750 a day at M12, so actual year-one revenue is well below run-rate, and spend as a share of year-one revenue is materially higher than 6%. The poster does not say so, and it shows no ROI or payback.
- The assumptions are not reconciled. "450 cars" is used in the budget against "590 registered" in cell 1, with no explanation.
- The innovation work is excluded: "Unpriced pilots: cost separately" (cell 9) leaves the AI, connected-care and package pilots outside the DKK30M.
- Incentives are tight. 150,000 first riders × the "≤DKK30" offer = DKK4.5M against "Incentives 4.6M / 15%". That leaves about DKK0.1M for the "referral credit" and the "Commuter pass test".
- The "People" panel gives FTE counts but no costs, so a reader cannot see how much of "Team/creative 3.4M" is staff and how much is agency.

### C5. KPIs & Success Metrics Aligned with Objectives: 7.5 / 10
(Cell 10 is read together with this criterion.)
**Strengths.**
- Five headline targets, each defined: "≥50% Aided awareness n=400 per wave", "150,000 First paid riders", "2.5M Paid trips a year run-rate by M12", "≥50% 90-day repeat†" with "†Full 90-day cohorts", and "≤DKK100 Media CAC per first rider" with "Media CAC = (search + social) / first paid riders".
- Service guard-rails: "≥95% completed", "≤2% service cancellations", "≥90% help within 24h".
- Interim gates: "M4 review ≥25,000 first paid riders · Media CAC ≤DKK120"; "M6 review ≥55,000 … ≥2,500 paid trips a day · ≥35% 90-day repeat".
- A B2B target: "≥100 business accounts by M12".
- A tracking cadence ("Weekly actions", "Monthly cohort review") and a decision rule ("M12: Renew · Revise · Stop").
- Cell 10 maps the objectives to the measures: "Become known locally … ≥50% aided awareness"; "Sustainable growth: Repeat + trips per car · Scale only through M4/M6 gates".

**Limits.**
- The business objectives are qualitative ("Stable local operation", "Become known locally", "Green & smart mobility", "Sustainable growth"). No revenue, market-share or profitability objective is set.
- No KPI measures the green objective (for example CO2 avoided or electric km), although "Green & smart mobility" is an objective and "Go Green" is the tagline.
- "Media CAC" leaves out "Screens/video 5.0M" and "Incentives 4.6M". Acquisition cost including those is about DKK144 per first rider, and the full budget is DKK200 per first rider. The headline "≤DKK100" therefore flatters acquisition efficiency.
- The channel KPIs in cell 5 ("installs", "CTR", "visits") have no targets. "trips/car" is named for the M12 review but has no target on the KPI panel.
- The size of "150,000 First paid riders" is not justified. It is 56% of the 269,277 focus segment and 27% of all 560,069 city adults, and the poster gives no catchment, visitor or business split to explain it.
- "Long-term sustainability and growth potential" (brief element 11) stops at M12. Nothing looks beyond the first year (for example a Netherlands or EU roll-out, or year-two economics).

- The pilots are listed, not shown working (no example offer, message or screen), and they are explicitly unbudgeted ("Unpriced pilots: cost separately").
- The campaign idea itself, "Copenhagen, meet Green SM", is an introduction line rather than a distinctive creative concept.

## 3. Strengths (max 5)
1. **The numbers hold together.** The budget lines sum to DKK30.0M and the shares to 100%. The caps (7.5 → 13.5, plus 16.5 held) sum to 30.0. The media budget, CAC gates and rider targets agree with each other: 40% × 7.5M = 3.0M = 25,000 × DKK120, and 12.0M / 150,000 = DKK80 ≤ DKK100.
2. **The plan is genuinely local.** It uses Statistics Denmark age data, the Danish keyword "bestil taxa", Danish landing copy, "København, mød Green SM", named Danish competitors (Drivr, Dantaxi, Viggo/Bolt, Taxa 4x27) and the current regulatory situation ("Dantaxi (part to be sold)").
3. **It is managed by stage gates.** "M1–3 Test / M4 / M6 gates / M7–12 Scale if OK", "Held at M6 DKK16.5M" and "M12: Renew · Revise · Stop". These show controlled scaling rather than spend that is committed up front.
4. **All eleven brief elements are covered**, in the brief's order and numbered, and every trend the brief names appears in cell 9.
5. **The concept is strong and on-brand.** The car-as-canvas idea, the Copenhagen setting and the brand palette give it a professional, distinctive look.

## 4. Weaknesses (max 7)
1. **The segmentation does not prioritise.** "2 All adults · M1+" overlaps the "Focus: 25–44 · M1+" segment and starts at the same time. There are no selection criteria, and the "57%" profile figure comes from "Capital Region · n=79 · ±11 pp", not from the focus segment.
2. **Positioning and branding do not line up.** The "Edge: Direct control → one service standard" is a supply-side claim, not a rider benefit. The tagline "Go Green / For a Green Future." leads on the green attribute that cell 3 lists as "Parity".
3. **There is no sales process for B2B, partners or visitors.** "B2B via LinkedIn (M4+) · partners (M6+)" is the whole of it, although DKK2.0M, 2 FTE and "≥100 business accounts" depend on it. "Visitors + airport" has no tactic.
4. **The budget's base and timing are inconsistent.** It is labelled "Objective-and-task" but sized as "6% of run-rate revenue", and the run-rate is an M12 end-state (about DKK493M), not year-one revenue. "450 cars" against "590 registered" is unexplained. The pilots are "Unpriced". The plan period "Nov 2026–Oct 2027" begins about three months after the stated launch, "Denmark 30/07/2026", yet the title is a "Launch" plan.
5. **The objectives and KPIs have gaps.** There is no revenue, ROI or green (CO2) KPI. "Media CAC" excludes screens and incentives (about DKK144 per rider including them, DKK200 per rider all-in). The size of the 150,000-rider target is not justified. The channel KPIs have no targets, and there is no view beyond M12.
6. **The brief is not fully met and the figures cannot all be traced.** No member contributions are shown, and there are no citations on the front, although the brief says to "Cite all your sources in the poster". The survey figures ("Denmark · n=1,005 · 70–90%", "n=79 · 57% / 22%", "Green by habit · 41%") cannot be matched to any entry in the reference list. The list is almost entirely company and web sources: one arXiv preprint, one OpenStax chapter and nothing from the module reading list.
7. **Legibility.** The smallest type would be about 2.5–3.5 mm high when printed, the cryptic shorthand needs a presenter, and space is lost to decoration while four cells are crowded.
