**Band: Distinction (70–79), at the low end.** I am confident the mark falls between 68 and 75. A credible marketing director would value the internal consistency of the numbers and the gated spending. The same director would challenge the customer-facing differentiation, the B2B sales process and the unit economics. Those three challenges keep the poster out of the upper Distinction range.

## 2. Per-criterion justification

### C1 Company Background and Country Context: 7.0 / 10
- **Strengths.** The poster covers origin, backing, model and footprint briefly: "Vingroup-backed", "Founded March 2023", "Owned fleet (590 registered) · Employed drivers", eight flags with "Vietnam 14/04/2023" marked as home and "Denmark 30/07/2026" highlighted, and "App-booked electric taxi rides · Denmark = 1st EU market". The VinFast car and the app → car icon make the product clear at a glance. Other cells add to the Danish context: Copenhagen population "1 Jul 2026" in C2, and the competitor structure in C3 ("Uber → Drivr · Dantaxi (part to be sold)", "Bolt → Viggo/Bolt · Taxa 4x27").
- **Limits.** The panel describes the company but does not analyse the Danish context. It gives no figure for the size of the Copenhagen taxi market, no regulatory note (taxi licensing, the employed-driver model) and no EV or charging context. "590 registered" has no place or date, so the reader cannot tell if it means the Danish fleet. Two claims are not reconciled: "Netherlands pilot" sits beside "Denmark = 1st EU market" with no dates to show which came first.

### C2 Target Market, Segmentation & Branding: 10.5 / 15
- **Strengths.**
  - The demographic base is real and quantified. The age-band chart reads "18–24 72,396 / 25–44 269,277 / 45–64 143,668 / 65+ 74,728", with "40.2% of city" and "City population (all ages): 670,389".
  - The psychographic labels are "Price-conscious · 57%", "Values trip control" and "Green by habit · 41%".
  - The behavioural labels are "Self-paying", "Existing taxi users" and "Recurring local trips".
  - Segments are prioritised and phased: "1 Focus: 25–44 · M1+ … 3 Business accounts · M4+ · 4 Visitors + airport · M6+".
  - Branding is specified fully: logo; "New Cyan #28bdbf Pantone 319 C"; "New Yellow #e3bb42"; "Xanh Display 2.0"; "Modern Liquid Glass UI/UX"; the message "Go Green For a Green Future."; and the campaign line localised as "Danish first: København, mød Green SM".
- **Limits.**
  - The segments overlap. "2 All adults · M1+" contains "1 Focus: 25–44 · M1+", so the group has phased its audiences but has not separated them.
  - The poster never shows why 25–44 is the focus. It gives no taxi-usage-by-age evidence, so the choice looks driven by population size.
  - Behavioural traits carry no numbers, and "Values trip control" has no figure.
  - The branding is the Vietnamese identity carried over almost unchanged. The brand message "Go Green For a Green Future." does not speak to the largest stated driver, price at 57%. Green is also a point of parity on the poster's own terms ("Parity: … electric options").
  - The positioning line "Electric rides. One managed experience." is clear. Its stated edge, "Direct control → one service standard", describes how the company operates. It is not a benefit a rider would notice or pay for.

### C3 Digital Marketing & Sales Strategies: 14.0 / 20
- **Strengths.**
  - The digital plan is practical and localised. The omnichannel path is "Omnichannel: page → app → ride". The Danish search term is "bestil taxa", and the landing page reads "Elektrisk taxa / Tjek prisen. / Book i appen.".
  - A channel-to-KPI table ties each channel to a measure: "Search: bestil taxa → installs · Social: Instagram · Facebook → CTR · City screens + video → awareness · Content: Copenhagen page → visits · CRM: reminders · referral → repeat".
  - The launch campaign is named ("Copenhagen, meet Green SM / city screens · video · social · search"). It is phased "M1–3 Test / M4 / M6 gates / M7–12 Scale if OK".
  - A controlled offer test is specified: "DKK15 vs DKK30 First paid ride / 1 per rider · ≤DKK30".
  - The sales funnel has five stages and a loop arrow back to the start. It runs from "Discover" to "First paid ride (Book now or pre-book 30 days)", "Experience", "Feedback (Invite unpaid reviews)" and "Repeat (Reminders · referral credit · Commuter pass test)".
  - The 4Ps summary in the side strip ("Price: Clear fare + trial") is light but correct.
- **Limits.**
  - The brief asks for "lead generation, prospecting, and conversion strategies". For B2B the poster gives one line, "Channels: app · B2B via LinkedIn (M4+) · partners (M6+)". It names no target account types, offer, pipeline stages or conversion step, and the partners are never named. This is weak given the DKK2.0M budget, the "2 FTE" and the "≥100 business accounts" KPI.
  - Influencer and advocacy activity is absent, although the course teaches it (week 8).
  - The choice of "Instagram · Facebook" for 25–44 Copenhageners is not explained.
  - The survey strip ("70–90% 0–1 taxi apps", "Usual app 22%") is never turned into a switching tactic. If most riders stay with one app, the plan should say how Green SM breaks that habit.

### C4 Budget & Resource Allocation: 11.5 / 15
- **Strengths.**
  - This is the strongest panel. The headline is "Proposed DKK30.0M · Nov 2026–Oct 2027 · excluding VAT". The allocation is a horizontal bar chart of eight lines with DKK and percentages, from "Search + app 7.0M / 23%" to "Contingency 2.0M / 7%".
  - The basis is stated: "*Run-rate, assumed: 450 cars × 15 trips/day × DKK200".
  - Spending is phased with release gates: "Budget reviews · cumulative caps / M4 review DKK7.5M → M6 review DKK13.5M / Held at M6 DKK16.5M".
  - The scope is explicit ("Outside budget: app build, cars, drivers, charging / frontline support"), and people are allocated ("Campaigns 3 FTE", "B2B sales 2 FTE").
  - Every figure reconciles (see §7). A 7% contingency and gated release are what a director would expect from a cautious market entry.
- **Limits.**
  - "Objective-and-task · 6% of run-rate revenue" combines two methods. The headline figure is set top-down as a share of sales; no task is costed bottom-up (for example, screen weeks from the referenced AFA JCDecaux price list, or a CPC × clicks estimate).
  - The 6% is taken on the M12 run-rate, not on year-1 revenue. Because trips ramp up, DKK30M is a much larger share of actual first-year revenue than the poster implies.
  - The innovation pilots are excluded ("Unpriced pilots: cost separately").
  - The fully loaded cost per first rider (DKK30.0M / 150,000 = DKK200) equals one average fare. The poster does not discuss payback.
  - The bars use an unlabelled 0–DKK8M scale. The 7.0M bar fills about 87% of its track, which a reader can mistake for a share of the total.

### C5 KPIs & Success Metrics Aligned with Objectives: 7.5 / 10
- **Strengths.**
  - Five headline targets have clear definitions: "≥50% Aided awareness n=400 per wave", "150,000 First paid riders", "2.5M Paid trips a year run-rate by M12", "≥50% 90-day repeat† (†Full 90-day cohorts)" and "≤DKK100 Media CAC per first rider".
  - The formula is stated: "Media CAC = (search + social) / first paid riders".
  - Service guardrails are set: "≥95% completed", "≤2% service cancellations", "≥90% help within 24h".
  - Interim gates: "M4 review ≥25,000 first paid riders · Media CAC ≤DKK120" and "M6 review ≥55,000 … ≥2,500 paid trips a day · ≥35% 90-day repeat".
  - B2B target: "≥100 business accounts by M12".
  - Tracking runs "M1–2 data checks · Ads → app → trips → help · Big Data dashboard · Weekly actions · Monthly cohort review", with the decision rule "M12: Renew · Revise · Stop". C10 links the KPIs back to the objectives ("Become known locally … ≥50% aided awareness").
- **Limits.**
  - There are no separately stated SMART marketing objectives. "COMMIT" targets do the work of objectives, which blurs the "aligned with objectives" test.
  - There is no financial or customer-value KPI: no revenue, contribution per trip, ROMI, CLV:CAC or payback.
  - There is no rating or NPS target, although "Feedback: Rating → fix issues" is a funnel stage.
  - The poster does not explain how first riders will be attributed to channels.
  - "Media CAC" excludes incentives and creative costs, so it flatters acquisition cost.

### C6 Creativity & Use of Emerging Concepts: 7.0 / 10
- **Strengths.** The poster covers every trend the brief names, each with a test or guardrail:
  - "AI targeting: Weather + night triggers · Consented app data · Timed offers"
  - "AI dispatch: Booking → AI match → taxi · Test vs local baseline · Wait time · fulfilment · Cancellations · Fairness"
  - "Connected care: One trip ID … Test FAQ bot → Human hand-off"
  - "Personalised packages: Pay-per-trip vs commuter monthly pass · Retention + margin · Stop if either falls" (subscription)
  - "Neuro test: calm-ride vs fare-first ads → attention · recall"
  - omnichannel in C5
  
  "PILOTS · Local performance untested" is an honest framing. The concept of the whole poster as a Green SM taxi in Copenhagen harbour is original and memorable.
- **Limits.**
  - Every innovation is labelled a pilot and none is costed ("Unpriced pilots: cost separately"), so they read as a list of options rather than part of the plan.
  - AI dispatch is an operations capability, not a marketing one.
  - Neuromarketing appears in a single footnote line.
  - The poster does not show what a rider would actually see, such as an example personalised offer or a pass price.

### C7 Visual Appeal & Presentation Quality: 15.0 / 20
- **Strengths.**
  - The concept is strong and the poster is colourful and on-brand: brand cyan and yellow throughout, a Danish flag and harbour setting, and the title "12-Month Marketing Plan for Electric Taxi Launch / Copenhagen, meet GREEN SM".
  - Ten numbered panels map to the brief's elements, with the identity cloud covering the eleventh.
  - Graphs and charts are used well: the population bar chart, survey bars with sample sizes, the budget bar chart and the KPI tiles. Icons and flags are used consistently.
- **Limits.**
  - Readability at pitch distance is the main problem. The plan content fills only about a third of the canvas; the sky, harbour and paving take the rest.
  - Body text is very small. In the 3,344-px render, cap height is about 10 px. That is about 3.6 mm at A0 and about 2.5 mm at A1, which cannot be read beyond arm's length.
  - Much of the copy is telegraphic and needs the presenter to decode it: "Parity: app booking · electric options · Dantaxi/Drivr fares", "Uber + Dantaxi 40–50%", "Taxa 4x27", "Xanh Display 2.0".
  - The panels follow the car's outline, which squeezes C4 and leaves an irregular white bulge in C8.
  - The identity cloud lists names and IDs but not who contributed which part. The brief asks for this, and the rubric's "Evident effort from all group members" depends on it.

## 3. Strengths (max 5)
1. **Market sizing is evidence-anchored.** The age-band chart uses official figures ("269,277 … 40.2% of city", "670,389"), and the survey bars disclose sample sizes and error ("Capital Region · n=79 · ±11 pp"). This is rare in group posters.
2. **The numbers form one consistent chain.** The run-rate assumption (450 × 15 × DKK200) drives the DKK30.0M budget, the "2.5M paid trips" KPI and the CAC gates, and every link reconciles (§7).
3. **The budget is gated and controlled.** It has eight lines, a 7% contingency, cumulative caps at M4 and M6, "Held at M6 DKK16.5M", and explicit exclusions.
4. **The KPIs are well built.** Each is defined, interim gates sit at M4 and M6, service guardrails are included, and the decision rule is "Renew · Revise · Stop".
5. **The Danish tactics are concrete and localised.** The search term is "bestil taxa", the landing page reads "Elektrisk taxa / Tjek prisen. / Book i appen.", the campaign line is "København, mød Green SM", and the offer test is "DKK15 vs DKK30".

## 4. Weaknesses (max 7)
1. **The differentiation is operational, not customer-facing.** "Parity: app booking · electric options · Dantaxi/Drivr fares" leaves only "Edge: Direct control → one service standard". The brand message "Go Green For a Green Future." does not answer the 57% "Price-conscious" segment, and green is parity.
2. **The B2B and partner sales process is missing.** "B2B via LinkedIn (M4+) · partners (M6+)" is all the poster gives to support DKK2.0M, 2 FTE and "≥100 business accounts by M12". There is no lead generation, prospecting or conversion step, which the brief asks for explicitly.
3. **Readability is poor for a poster.** Content fills roughly a third of the canvas, body text is about 3.6 mm at A0, and much of the copy is cryptic shorthand. Readers will depend on the presenter.
4. **The budget method and cost base are thin.** "Objective-and-task" is asserted but the figure is set top-down at 6% of M12 run-rate. No line is costed bottom-up. Pilots are "Unpriced". The fully loaded cost per first rider is DKK200, one average fare, and payback is not discussed.
5. **The target and segmentation logic are shaky.** "150,000 First paid riders" equals 26.8% of Copenhagen's 560,069 adults and 55.7% of the 25–44 focus group, with no bridge showing where they come from. Segments 1 and 2 overlap, and the focus on 25–44 is not evidenced.
6. **There is no financial or customer-value outcome KPI.** The poster has no revenue, contribution, ROMI, CLV:CAC or rating/NPS target. "Media CAC" excludes incentives.
7. **Compliance and long-term gaps remain.**
   - Member contributions are not shown.
   - The front has no visible citations, although the brief says "Cite all your sources in the poster".
   - The pack calls the render A0, but the brief specifies an "A1 Paper Chart"; the group should confirm the size.
   - The alignment panel does not look beyond the 12 months (no Denmark scale-up, further Nordic cities or the Netherlands in year two). The rubric itself has no "Alignment" criterion, but the brief asks for long-term growth potential.

## 5. Three changes worth the most marks for the least effort (ranked)
1. **Add a contribution line to the identity cloud** (for example "Giao: 1–4 · Khoi: 5–7 · Vy: 8–10 + references", with the real split). This is one line of text. It answers a brief requirement and the rubric's "Evident effort from all group members", and it removes a compliance risk. **Estimated gain: +0.5 to +1.0 (C7).**
2. **Add a five-step B2B pipeline to C6.** The steps would be: named target account types (for example hotels, corporate HQs, airport desk) → LinkedIn plus direct outreach → trial business account → invoiced account → quarterly review, with one conversion figure, and the M6+ partners named. This uses existing space in the "Channels" footer and fixes the largest gap in the 20%-weighted criterion. **Estimated gain: +1.0 (C3).**
3. **Turn the edge into a rider benefit and fix the segment overlap.** Replace "Edge: Direct control → one service standard" with a felt benefit and its proof, for example "Same fair fare, same trained driver standard, every ride: every car electric, every driver employed". Add one price-led line under "Go Green". Relabel segment 2 as "Other adults (18–24, 45+)". **Estimated gain: +0.5 to +1.0 (C2).**

(Next in line: a sixth KPI tile such as "fully loaded cost per first rider ≤DKK200 / payback in n trips", and relabelling the budget method "6% of M12 run-rate, checked against task costs". Estimated gain +0.5 across C4/C5.)

## 6. Likely Q&A questions (max 5)
1. "Your own poster says fares and electric options are at parity with Dantaxi/Drivr. Why would a price-conscious 30-year-old with one taxi app switch? What does 'direct control' mean to the rider?"
2. "150,000 first paid riders is over a quarter of Copenhagen's adults and more than half of your 25–44 focus group. How many come from residents, visitors/airport and business accounts, and what is the evidence?"
3. "DKK30M is 6% of a run-rate you only reach at M12. What share of actual year-1 revenue is it? With a fully loaded DKK200 per first rider, roughly one fare, how many repeat trips do you need to pay back?"
4. "Green SM launched in Denmark on 30/07/2026, but your 'launch campaign' starts in November. What happens from August to October? And was the Netherlands pilot before or after Denmark, the '1st EU market'?"
5. "How will two B2B sellers win 100 accounts? Who are the targets, what is the offer, and what does each account cost at DKK2.0M / 100 = DKK20,000?"

### 7b. Internal arithmetic: checks that reconcile
- **Budget lines.** 7.0 + 5.0 + 5.0 + 4.6 + 3.4 + 2.0 + 1.0 + 2.0 = **30.0** ✓. The percentages (23.3, 16.7, 16.7, 15.3, 11.3, 6.7, 3.3, 6.7) round to 23/17/17/15/11/7/3/7 and sum to 100 ✓.
- **Release gates.** "Held at M6 DKK16.5M" = 30.0 − 13.5 ✓.
- **Run-rate.** 450 × 15 × DKK200 × 365 = DKK492.75M, and 6% = DKK29.6M. "DKK30.0M" is therefore 6.09%, an acceptable rounding ✓.
- **Trips.** 450 × 15 × 365 = 2.46M trips, which supports "2.5M Paid trips a year run-rate by M12" ✓.
- **M6 ramp.** "≥2,500 paid trips a day" is about 5.6 trips per car per day, against 15 at run-rate. This is a coherent ramp ✓.
- **Media CAC.** At M12, (7.0 + 5.0) / 150,000 = DKK80, within "≤DKK100" ✓. At M4, a 40% search-plus-social share of the DKK7.5M cap gives DKK3.0M / 25,000 = DKK120, exactly matching "Media CAC ≤DKK120" ✓. At M6, 40% of 13.5M / 55,000 = DKK98 ✓.
- **Incentives.** 150,000 × "≤DKK30" = DKK4.5M, within "Incentives 4.6M" ✓.
- **Population.** 269,277 / 670,389 = 40.17%, so "40.2% of city" is correct on an all-ages base ✓.
- **Bar charts.** The population bars are roughly proportional. The budget bars are drawn to a 0–8M scale (7.0M ≈ 87%, 4.6M ≈ 57%, 1.0M ≈ 12%) ✓.

### 7c. Internal inconsistencies and tensions to resolve
1. **Fleet: "Owned fleet (590 registered)" against the run-rate assumption of "450 cars"** (76%). The 140-car gap is unexplained. It could be spares or maintenance, but the poster should say so.
2. **Base for "40.2% of city".** The chart is labelled "18+" and its four bands sum to 560,069 adults. On that base, 25–44 is 48.1%. The 40.2% uses the all-ages total of 670,389, so the denominator differs from the chart's. Relabel the figure "40.2% of all residents".
3. **CAC definition and budget label.** The formula says "Media CAC = (search + social) / first paid riders", but the budget line is "Search + app". It is unclear whether app-store spend is inside the CAC. The ≤DKK100 target holds either way (DKK80).
4. **Incentives headroom.** 150,000 × DKK30 = DKK4.5M leaves only DKK0.1M of the 4.6M line for the "referral credit" and "Commuter pass test" in C6. If the DKK30 arm wins, those are effectively unfunded.
5. **Overlapping segments.** "1 Focus: 25–44" sits inside "2 All adults", so the segments are not mutually exclusive.
6. **"Denmark = 1st EU market" against "Netherlands pilot".** The order is not shown, and the claim needs a date.
7. **Launch timing.** "Denmark 30/07/2026" against a plan dated "Nov 2026–Oct 2027" and titled "Electric Taxi Launch", with a "LAUNCH CAMPAIGN" in M1–3. The gap between operational launch and marketing launch is not explained.
8. **Realism, not arithmetic.** "150,000 First paid riders" equals 26.8% of the city's 560,069 adults and 55.7% of the 25–44 group, alongside "70–90% 0–1 taxi apps" (low multi-app use). This is internally possible only if visitors and business accounts contribute heavily, and the poster does not show that split.
9. **Format.** The pack calls the render A0, but the brief specifies "One Standard Size A1 Paper Chart". At A1, body text falls to about 2.5 mm cap height.
