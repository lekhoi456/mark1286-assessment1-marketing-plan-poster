## Criterion-by-criterion rationale

### 1. Company Background and Country Context — 8.5/10

**Credit.** Section 1 clearly identifies Green SM, its Vietnamese origin, foundation in March 2023, the owned-fleet/employed-driver operating model, international presence and Copenhagen service. The closing line, “Green SM Taxi: app-booked electric taxi rides in Copenhagen”, states the relevant offer directly. Vietnam and Denmark launch dates appear beside their flags. The Copenhagen setting and Denmark flags maintain a coherent destination-market context throughout the design.

**Marks withheld.** The country-context treatment is mainly geographic and chronological. Section 1 does not explain the commercial implications of operating in Denmark; local consumer and competitor context appears elsewhere but remains brief. The flag arrangement also communicates the wider footprint more readily than the status or chronology of each market. This is a clear introduction, with less contextual depth than a near-perfect award would require. No assertion is made here that an external company claim is factually false: external source contents were not supplied for verification.

### 2. Target Market, Segmentation & Branding — 11.5/15

**Credit.** Section 2 specifies “Copenhagen residents aged 25–44”, then adds behavioural criteria: self-paying, existing taxi users and recurring local trips. Its proposed psychographic profile includes price consciousness, trip control and fair treatment. The age comparison is quantified, dated and geographically labelled. The 40.2% figure is consistent with 269,277 divided by the stated all-age population of 670,389. Section 3 distinguishes a directly managed service from partner-operated rides and explicitly acknowledges common features, “App booking · Electric options”. Section 4 offers a coherent logo, message, colour system, typeface and Danish-first language policy.

**Marks withheld.** A large population segment is not itself evidence of an attractive taxi segment. The poster does not establish why 25–44-year-olds offer stronger taxi frequency, willingness to pay, switching potential or contribution than the alternatives shown. “Proposed rider profile” correctly marks assumptions, but leaves the psychographic case largely untested. The service area is mentioned without a geographic definition. The positioning mechanism, “Direct control → one service standard”, is intelligible, but the benefit is not supported by a concrete service guarantee or a demonstrated gap in competitor performance. Brand presentation is strong; the commercial justification for prioritisation is less developed.

### 3. Digital Marketing & Sales Strategies — 15/20

**Credit.** Sections 5 and 6 connect acquisition to service delivery and repeat purchase. “Copenhagen page → Green SM app → Ride + help” gives an understandable channel journey, supported by consistent fares, offers and help. The first-paid-ride experiment, “10% vs 20%”, is substantially more practical than simply naming a promotional discount: it has a DKK30 maximum, a 400-rider cap and a one-per-rider restriction. Section 6 covers discovery, fare checking, first completed trip, experience, feedback and repeat purchase. The proposed data process includes opt-in and human review. M1–2 verification, M3–6 testing and conditional M7–12 scaling provide a basic timetable.

**Marks withheld.** Search, SEO and social are mostly channel labels. There is no illustrative search term, social platform, content example or message for the selected audience. Consequently the rubric's requirement for relevant practical examples is only partly met by the voucher experiment and customer journey. The “30-day paid” outcome is not self-explanatory enough to establish the winning test condition. The survey fragments “40–50% one app”, “Lower price 57%” and “Usual app 22%” identify sample sizes but omit the precise question or behavioural situation being measured. This limits the reader's ability to follow the evidence-to-tactic argument. The sales sequence is coherent but lacks conversion assumptions between stages.

### 4. Budget & Resource Allocation — 11/15

**Credit.** Section 7 presents a legible visual allocation for DKK600,000, explicitly covering 12 months and excluding VAT. The seven amounts total DKK600,000 and the displayed percentages total 100%, allowing for rounding. Team cost is explained as 460 hours × DKK600, and the four work allocations sum to 460 hours. The voucher allowance of DKK12,000 is consistent with 400 riders receiving no more than DKK30 each. Reviews distinguish cumulative commitments from the held balance: DKK265,000 plus DKK335,000 reconciles at M6. Cars, drivers, charging and frontline support are explicitly outside this marketing budget; their exclusion is not an arithmetic error.

**Marks withheld.** Feasibility is asserted more than demonstrated. Search and social spending is not connected to reach, clicks, booking conversion or the 1,200 first-paid-rider target through any planning assumptions. The precise screen amount has no visible number of sites, duration or campaign package. The labour rate is honestly a “Planning allowance”, with limited justification for the quantity or capacity supplied. Sections 6 and 9 explicitly leave several proposed pilots unpriced. That transparency earns credit, but the plan still cannot establish what delivering the complete proposal would cost. The concern is incomplete costing of selected marketing/service innovations, not a requirement to include all fleet operations in a marketing budget.

### 5. KPIs & Success Metrics Aligned with Objectives — 8/10

**Credit.** Section 8 supplies a strong measurable dashboard: +10 percentage points consideration, 1,200 distinct first paid riders, 2,500 paid rides, at least 50% 90-day repeat and no more than DKK165 cost per first paid rider. Paid campaign cohorts are separated from organic/referral upside; full 90-day cohorts are specified. Service indicators address completion, cancellation and response time. M4/M6 checkpoints and weekly/monthly review routines make monitoring actionable. Section 10 links consideration, paid trial, service, repeat and contribution to the M12 renew/revise/stop decision.

**Marks withheld.** The consideration survey has a start/end schedule but no baseline level, sampling plan or stated population. Cost per first paid rider does not define its cost numerator. Search plus social equals DKK198,000, which divided by 1,200 produces exactly DKK165; including vouchers would give DKK175. This is an ambiguity requiring definition, not proof that the target is arithmetically wrong. Contribution is central to sustainable growth and package decisions but has no quantified threshold. Data sources are named, but the operational attribution and deduplication rules are not visible. These gaps prevent a fully auditable evaluation design.

### 6. Creativity & Use of Emerging Concepts — 8/10

**Credit.** Section 9 goes beyond listing fashionable terms. AI dispatch has a booking-to-match mechanism and named evaluation outcomes; connected care links app, vehicle/driver and support through one trip ID; the FAQ bot includes human hand-off. Personalised packages are conditional on frequency and contribution, compare pay-per-trip with prepaid/monthly forms, and stop if retention or margin falls. “PROPOSED PILOTS · Local performance untested” keeps proposals distinct from observed results. Combined with the voucher comparison and the unified taxi-shaped poster, there is substantial creative effort.

**Marks withheld.** AI targeting and “Big Data + AI” remain at a conceptual level: the necessary data, useful scale and advantage over simpler targeting are unspecified. Dispatch, chatbot and subscription proposals are explicitly unpriced, limiting how convincingly they are incorporated into the financed plan. Their originality is principally in the combination and local proposal, rather than a demonstrated novel market mechanism. The rubric gives examples of emerging concepts; it does not require every example to appear, so no deduction is made simply for an absent concept such as neuromarketing.

### 7. Visual Appeal & Presentation Quality — 15/20

**Credit.** The car-shaped composition is distinctive and closely connected to the product. Copenhagen scenery, cyan/yellow branding, numbered white panels and consistent iconography create a coherent visual identity. The age chart, budget bars, journey, dashboard and innovation diagrams communicate information visually. Names and student numbers appear in the upper-right cloud. All eleven requested content categories are visibly represented when the identity block and ten numbered content panels are considered together. A substantial reference list is visibly included at the bottom.

**Marks withheld.** The full view gives very large areas to the vehicle, wheels and scenery while some of the most assessable material occupies compact cells. Section 8 and the lower notes in Sections 5, 7, 9 and 10 demand close inspection; the reference strip is particularly small. Visual integration is excellent, but classroom-distance information access is weaker. The body contains no visible author-date or numbered links tying specific figures and claims to the reference list, so attribution remains hard to follow even though references are present. The graphic style is consistent, but the artefact alone does not establish hand drawing or evident effort by every named member; those components cannot receive verified performance credit from the supplied evidence.

## Overall judgement

This is a coherent, visually accomplished and substantially complete marketing-plan poster. Its strongest academic features are the linked acquisition–service–retention logic, explicit paid-cohort monitoring, internally consistent budget arithmetic, and conditional treatment of innovation. It avoids presenting proposed local outcomes as achieved results.

The principal barrier to a higher mark is the gap between a persuasive framework and a demonstrated feasible plan. Segment attractiveness is inferred from population and proposed behaviours; digital execution lacks specific platform/content examples; spend is not converted into acquisition expectations; and several innovations remain outside the costed proposal. The poster's rich illustration also compresses detail that an examiner needs to read.

A mark of **77/100** recognises substantial quality without treating completeness, strong design or cautious wording as substitutes for commercial justification and evaluability.
