# Independent assessment — Examiner 1

## Decision

**Poster-only mark: 78/100. Confidence: moderate.**

This is a coherent, visually distinctive marketing plan with a particularly strong connection between acquisition, service quality, repeat use and staged investment. It earns substantial credit against every rubric criterion. The principal limitations are the evidence supporting target prioritisation, the operational specificity of channel execution, incomplete substantiation of financial assumptions, and the small size of much of the assessed content within a highly decorative composition.

This is an independent assessment of the supplied artefact, not a confirmed mark for the live group presentation. No presentation, Q&A, physical chart, contribution record or submission evidence was supplied.

## Materials and method

Only the following were examined:

- `00_brief_and_criteria/source/mark1286-assessment-1-brief.txt`.

The student's identity details occupy a separate upper-right cloud. These collectively address the eleven content elements in the brief; the presence of ten numbered panels is not an omission.

No other poster version, project commentary, previous review, evidence register or copy master was used. The bibliography was assessed as visible content; its external sources were not opened or independently verified. Therefore, credit for the contextual information concerns its clarity, relevance and visible attribution, not a claim that every factual statement has been independently established.

## Criterion-by-criterion rationale

### 1. Company Background and Country Context — 8.5/10

**Credit.** Panel 1 provides the founding date, Vingroup backing, Vietnam origin, a geographical footprint and the Netherlands pilot distinction. The line “Green SM Taxi: app-booked electric taxi rides in Copenhagen” states the actual service and proposed local context clearly. “Owned fleet • Employed drivers” adds an operational feature relevant to the subsequent positioning. Denmark receives a visible highlight and launch date. Panel 3 adds a local competitive frame rather than relying solely on global company history.

**Marks withheld.** The country information is more a footprint and date summary than an explanation of why Copenhagen is attractive or difficult for this particular business. Local demand and competitor information exist elsewhere, but the commercial implications of entering an established taxi market are only partly developed. The founding and service-launch dates are distinguishable, but the dates printed beneath flags would benefit from explicit event labels. These are limitations in depth and immediate clarity, not evidence that the dates are wrong.

### 2. Target Market, Segmentation & Branding — 12/15

**Credit.** Panel 2 gives a precise demographic and geographical segment: Copenhagen residents aged 25–44. It includes age-group population comparisons, “269,277”, “40.2% of city” and the all-age denominator “670,389”. Behavioural filters are explicit: self-paying, existing taxi users and recurring local trips. The proposed psychographic profile includes price consciousness, trip control and fair treatment. Panel 4 establishes a consistent logo, cyan/yellow palette, message, type and interface direction; “Danish first • English second” demonstrates local adaptation. Panel 3 recognises that app booking and electric options are shared features and instead proposes operational control as the differentiator.

**Marks withheld.** A large population segment is not itself evidence of the most attractive taxi demand. The poster does not demonstrate why 25–44-year-olds should take priority over older customers, nor estimate the subset who satisfy its behavioural filters. “Proposed rider profile” appropriately admits an assumption but leaves psychographic validation unresolved. The green brand promise is consistent across the poster, although its particular appeal to a price-conscious audience is less explicitly developed than its corporate visual identity. The promise “More reliable” remains a company promise rather than demonstrated comparative performance.

### 3. Digital Marketing & Sales Strategies — 15.5/20

**Credit.** Panels 5–6 offer a practical customer journey: search/social discovery, fare checking, app booking, completed first ride, experience, feedback and repeat. The Copenhagen page → app → ride/help flow makes the omnichannel idea concrete, including consistent fares, offers and help. The first-ride test is unusually specific for a poster: “10% vs 20%”, maximum DKK30, 400-rider cap and one voucher per rider. Opt-in reminders and monthly bundle pilots address retention. The plan includes a phased twelve-month sequence and review points rather than presenting a channel list alone. Local research is visibly differentiated between Denmark, n=1,005, and Capital Region, n=79.

**Marks withheld.** Search, SEO and social are named without worked examples of a search query, content theme, social placement, audience/creative pairing or execution frequency. The reader cannot judge the channel allocation from an expected reach-to-booking funnel. The research labels “40–50% one app”, “Lower price 57%” and “Usual app 22%” omit the survey question and exact response context, making the strategic inference less self-contained. The first-ride experiment names offer variants but its “30-day paid” outcome and winning criterion are not fully defined. These gaps constrain the claim of a fully practical execution plan, while the conversion and retention mechanisms themselves are strong.

### 4. Budget & Resource Allocation — 11.5/15

**Credit.** Panel 7 is a well-organised visual allocation. The seven amounts sum exactly to DKK600,000; the displayed percentages sum to 100% after rounding. Team cost is transparent: 460 hours × DKK600/hour = DKK276,000, with the hours allocated across four tasks. The DKK12,000 voucher allowance covers 400 riders at the stated maximum DKK30. Contingency is explicitly included. Cumulative M4 and M6 commitments and the DKK335,000 held at M6 create a sensible staged funding mechanism. Twelve-month scope and VAT exclusion are explicit.

**Marks withheld.** Numerical consistency is stronger than demonstrated realism. The hourly rate is labelled a planning allowance, and the poster provides no comparable assumption basis for paid search, social or tools. The precise DKK43,195 screens allocation has a potentially relevant rate-card reference, but no visible package, duration or quantity calculation. Dispatch, chatbot and subscription pilots are expressly unpriced in Panel 9 despite forming part of the proposed strategy. Their disclosure is good practice, but it leaves the cost of delivering the full proposal unresolved. Excluding cars, drivers and charging is reasonable for a marketing budget; the assessment does not require treating their omission as an arithmetic error. The remaining weakness is the bridge between marketing investment, achievable acquisition and commercial contribution.

### 5. KPIs & Success Metrics Aligned with Objectives — 8/10

**Credit.** Panel 8 provides twelve-month targets for consideration, distinct first paid riders, paid rides, repeat and acquisition cost. The paid campaign cohort is differentiated from organic/referral upside. “Full 90-day cohorts” is an important retention qualification. Service completion, cancellations and help-response targets connect the “one managed experience” positioning with operations. M4/M6 thresholds and weekly/monthly review rhythms make evaluation actionable. Panel 10 connects this measurement to renewal, revision or stopping, and to repeat use before expansion.

**Marks withheld.** The consideration survey lacks a defined sampling approach and survey population. Baseline establishment is scheduled, which is sensible, but the choice of target magnitudes is not justified. The cost-per-first-paid-rider numerator is not explicitly defined. Search plus social spending totals DKK198,000; divided by 1,200 riders this gives DKK165, so a media-only interpretation is plausible. The complete budget divided by the same cohort is DKK500; these are different measures, not an arithmetic contradiction. The poster should distinguish them to avoid overstating acquisition efficiency. “Contribution” is part of the final business review but has no visible numerical target or measurement definition.

### 6. Creativity & Use of Emerging Concepts — 7.5/10

**Credit.** Panel 9 applies AI targeting, AI dispatch, connected care and personalised packages to recognisable customer or operational tasks. The “One trip ID” diagram explains the connected service concept particularly well. Consent, human hand-off, comparison against a local baseline and the instruction to stop packages if retention or margin falls show critical judgement. Panel 5 connects customer data and reminders with the broader customer journey. “PROPOSED PILOTS • Local performance untested” clearly distinguishes proposals from demonstrated success.

**Marks withheld.** Several ideas remain pilot concepts rather than fully specified innovations. Data readiness, ownership, implementation resources and the path from pilot to routine delivery are largely absent. Some of the largest technical interventions are outside the priced plan. Consequently, the poster demonstrates good application and caution, but not yet compelling feasibility or highly distinctive innovation in the marketing offer itself. No deduction is made merely because it does not include every emerging concept listed as an example in the brief.

### 7. Visual Appeal & Presentation Quality — 15/20

**Credit.** The Copenhagen cityscape and electric taxi create a strong, immediately relevant visual identity. The numbered cells provide a clear reading order. Consistent symbols, arrows, population bars, budget bars, KPI cards and compact process diagrams communicate substantive information rather than decorating paragraphs. Contrast within the white content panels is strong. The header identifies the university, brand and plan; the identity cloud contains three names and student numbers. A substantial reference list is present.

**Marks withheld.** The large vehicle, wheels, sky and scenery occupy substantial space, forcing much of the assessed explanation into small panels. The hierarchy is attractive in overview, but the evidence and qualifications depend on close reading; the reference strip is particularly small. Panel 6 and the lower lines of Panels 5, 8, 9 and 10 contain dense, small text despite generous decorative space elsewhere. The bibliography is not connected to specific claims through visible author-date citations or keyed markers, weakening immediate source traceability. “Copenhagen meet” is also less polished than the rest of the title treatment. These are visible communication limitations; I do not claim to have tested a printed poster at viewing distance.

The rubric also refers to hand-drawn visuals and evident effort from all group members. A digital SVG can display a hand-drawn visual style, but it cannot establish that a physical chart was hand drawn or that every group member participated. Names alone establish identification, not contribution. Neither full compliance nor misconduct is inferred from the artefact. The brief specifies A1, while the SVG declares 1189 × 841 mm; the handbook specifies a standard-size chart without naming A1. This is a format discrepancy to resolve, not a basis for an invented automatic mark cap.

## Most important strengths

1. A connected strategy from local discovery and trial through service quality to repeat use.
2. Strong visual explanation of the target audience, customer journey, budget and review decisions.
3. Arithmetically coherent marketing budget with staged commitments and a held reserve.
4. Explicit distinction between proposed concepts, untested outcomes and operational targets.

## Main reasons this does not receive a higher mark

1. Target prioritisation and channel choices are plausible but incompletely demonstrated.
2. Acquisition economics, pilot costs and the business-contribution test are not fully joined together.
3. Channel execution and experiment success rules need greater specificity.
4. The decorative composition constrains the size and immediate readability of assessed content.

## Limits and unresolved questions

- Actual presentation delivery, timing, Q&A quality and all-member participation cannot be assessed from the SVG.
- Physical hand-drawn production, final printed readability, accepted paper size and Moodle submission cannot be established.
- Bibliographic presence does not independently verify the underlying claims or the accuracy of competitor descriptions.

**Status: DONE_WITH_CONCERNS** — the requested independent poster assessment is complete; the concerns are the stated limits of an artefact-only judgement.
