## 1. Overall judgement

The copy answers all eleven brief elements under the exact headings and names a module concept in almost every panel. Budget lines, shares, targets and gate figures all reproduce from the approved model. Twenty-two sourced claims hold at the cited PDF page; one locator is incomplete. Marks are most at risk where a marker ticks the brief's questions and finds no visible answer (UVP, sales channel, segment comparison, neuromarketing, revenue model), plus a printed evidence code and an unresourced help route. Provisional total about 67/100 (range 58–75); C7 is the largest unknown.

## 3. Ranked findings

### Blocking

**B1. Internal code printed.** “Evidence: E-029” in the Street screens and Screen setup rows. C4.2; X6, R91. Risk: a code where a citation belongs. Revision: “Rate card (AFA Decaux, 2026)”.

### Significant

**S1. Help route promised, not resourced.** “problems reach a staffed help route”; “CRM and help route | 72000 | 12 | Assumption: 120 hours”. C4.3, C3.3. Plan §8–9 puts frontline support outside this budget. Risk: 2.3 hours a week reads as the whole support desk. Revision: rename “CRM set-up and help-route handover”; add “Cars, drivers, charging and frontline support are operating costs outside this budget.”

**S2. No KPI tests the promise.** KPI table; “service targets met”. C5.1. Plan §10 has a clarity score and a help-route standard, which Gate A requires. Risk: the core promise is never measured. Revision: add “Riders who found area and fare clear | O4 service | Set from month 1–2 baseline | In-app survey | Monthly”; name the Gate A targets.

**S3. Segmentation asserted, not shown.** “we segment Copenhagen taxi demand by who pays and why people ride”. C2.1; brief: “How did you segment and prioritize”. Risk: prioritisation looks unexamined. Revision (plan §4): “Not targeted: organisations (account invoicing not evidenced in Denmark) and visitors (rides rarely repeat).” Shorten the persona to fit.

**S4. UVP unnamed; differentiation elsewhere.** “Positioning statement: ... because it owns its cars and employs its drivers.” C2.2; the brief asks for the “unique value proposition (UVP)”. KFST p. 174 (para 850) contrasts Green SM with dispatch offices whose taxis are run by self-employed operators. Risk: the UVP question looks unanswered. Revision: label “UVP:” and add “Unlike existing dispatch offices, whose taxis are run by self-employed operators, Green SM employs its drivers (Konkurrence- og Forbrugerstyrelsen, 2026b, p. 174).” Shorten the ACEA sentence to fit.

**S5. “Price is level” overstated.** “Benefit positioning, because price is level.” C2.2. Green SM Denmark ApS (no date b) p. 3 lists higher app-price maxima; evidence note C-004: DKK227 is never an observed app fare. Risk: the service is app-booked, so a checker can dispute parity. Revision: “because published meter prices are level. The app shows the fare before booking (Green SM Denmark ApS, no date b).” (step 2, same page).

**S6. No sales channel.** “Sales process: for a taxi, the sale is a completed, paid trip.” names no channel. C3.2, R50; brief element 7: “What sales channels”. Risk: a named brief question unanswered. Revision: “Channel: direct to consumer through Green SM's own app” (plan §8; sales-channels).

**S7. Trend list partly answered.** “Deferred on purpose: AI chatbots ... and a subscription model”. C6.2; the brief lists “neuro marketing, subscription and revenue model”. Risk: two listed trends silently skipped. Revision (plan §11): “Revenue model: pay per trip (usage-based). Neuromarketing: not used; surveys measure clarity.” Give AI its test condition: “after a human-run FAQ shows which questions recur”.

**S8. Company panel thin.** “Green SM began in Vietnam, where its Xanh SM taxi service launched in Hanoi”. C1.1–C1.2. Registered evidence: green-sm-nd p. 2 (founding announced by Vingroup's chairman; “electric cars and electric motorbikes”); KFST p. 134 (Uber and Dantaxi 40–50% of Greater Copenhagen taxi revenue, January–May 2025). Risk: “core products/services” and context read thin. Revision: one sentence each within the 15 spare words.

### Minor

- M1 (C2, X2). “(n = 79) ... drove the last app choice ... pp. 150, 194”: n is printed on p. 151, and the question asked why riders chose a platform app over a classic taxi company. Use “pp. 150–151, 194” and “top reasons for choosing a platform app”; add “in Denmark” to the E-023 figure. Risk: a checker cannot find n.
- M2 (C3). “Awareness:” is not one of the module's five decision stages (w03-lecture s5); label it “Before the need (awareness)”. Risk: concept misapplied.
- M3 (C4.1–C4.2). Budget table: add “(DKK)”, thousands separators and one decimal (6.4 and 0.8 still total 100.0). Show the fare behind “recover about DKK46,000”: DKK227 × 30% = DKK68 a ride. Risk: figure unverifiable.
- M4 (C4.3). “in-car help card” and “Resident panel survey” have no cost line; A9 funds the card from contingency after a quote. Say so in the Contingency basis. Risk: unfunded activities.
- M5 (C5.3). “at most DKK1,829” beside the “DKK524” target: add “Gate floors are the downside case.” Risk: reads as inconsistent.
- M6 (C1–C2, X6). Wording: “a city of 670,389 people” → “Copenhagen Municipality”; “ACEA (2026) reports that 68.5%” → “figures give” (calculated); “installers” → “people who installed the app”; ranges need en dashes. Risk: small accuracy slips.

### Defensible challenges

- “easy to judge before you book” is not unique: a dispatch office told KFST that Uber's app gives a “prisestimat før bestilling” (p. 150), and TAXA's app calculates “en fast pris” (TAXA 4x35 p. 2). Lead the UVP with employed drivers and help afterwards.
- Psychographic is hypothesis only; KFST p. 151 shows 11% chose an app for “øget tryghed og sikkerhed”. Partial support; register it before use.
- “recover about DKK46,000” may read as a weak case; add the capped exposure (DKK177,000 if Gate A fails).
- Known issues: unpartnered year suffixes may suggest missing references; the missing contribution statement threatens C7's “effort from all group members”.
