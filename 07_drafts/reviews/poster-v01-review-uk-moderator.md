# Poster v01: mock UK-moderator review

Draft: `07_drafts/poster-v01-2026-09-28.md` (1,362 panel words by `wordcount.py`, plus about 280 reference words), read as a moderator would, without the pitch. Scores are estimates, not official bands or the real marker's view. KFST = Konkurrence- og Forbrugerstyrelsen (2026b).

## 1. Overall judgement

The poster is honest and traceable: every claim checked matches its source, the budget reconciles to the approved plan, and assumptions, hypothesis and persona are labelled. Its weak point is the central promise, "easy to judge before you book and easy to get help from afterwards": price parity is narrower than stated, the plan leaves the help route unfunded, and no KPI measures clarity or help. Without the pitch, three links are missing: why clarity answers a price-and-habit market, why this segment, and why spend DKK600,000 to recover DKK46,000. Most fixes reuse approved-plan wording.

## 3. Findings

### Blocking

**B1 (C2, defect). "Benefit positioning, because price is level."** Evidence: E-015 p. 3 gives DKK227 for the day meter, but the same page publishes an app maximum (about DKK329 for this trip) and the service is app-booked. Risk: an overclaim at the core of the positioning. Revise: "because meter prices are level", then add "App fares have a separate published maximum, so we promise clear fare terms, not a lower price" (plan §5).

**B2 (C5, C3, defect). The promise is asserted, not secured or measured.** Quotes: "problems reach a staffed help route"; O4 KPIs cover only completion and "Cancellations caused by Green SM"; Gate A "service targets met". Evidence: plan §8 puts support staff outside this budget; plan §10 help-route and clarity KPIs were dropped. Risk: nothing shows how the positioning is proved. Revise: add rows "Help requests answered on time / O4 / standard set with operations, months 1-2 / support log / weekly" and "Riders who found area and fare clear / O4 / set after baseline / post-trip question / monthly"; Gate A "service and help-route standards met"; Sales step 4 "a help route staffed outside this budget, advertised only once its standard is met".

### Significant

**S1 (C2). "price (57%) and habit (22%) drove the last app choice."** Evidence: KFST Table 5.12 (pp. 150–151; n = 79 on p. 151) covers Capital Region residents who chose Uber, Bolt or TaxiToGo over a traditional taxi firm. Risk: price and habit drive choice, the positioning sells clarity, and nothing bridges them. Revise: state that population, and make the psychographic line "because prices are level, we expect them to switch only if area and fare are easy to check and help is easy to get."

**S2 (C2). "segment … by who pays and why people ride, then use concentrated targeting on one segment."** Evidence: the brief asks how segments were prioritised; plan §4 names alternatives. Risk: no rejected segment is visible. Add: "residents paying for their own trips, organisations and visitors. We target residents: organisation accounts are not evidenced in Denmark and visitor trips seldom repeat (our judgement)."

**S3 (C4). "at an assumed 30% contribution per ride … recover about DKK46,000 … a capped market-entry investment".** Evidence: 676 × DKK227 × 30% = DKK46,036; model break-even 8,811 rides; exposure DKK177,000 if Gate A fails. Risk: "30% of what" and "capped at what" are unanswered. Revise: "30% of a DKK227 trip … about DKK46,000 of DKK600,000; break-even needs about 8,800 rides. A failed Gate A stops spending at DKK177,000."

**S4 (C5). "repeat use at least 20% and cost per new rider at most DKK1,829 to continue, and 35% to release street screens."** Evidence: DKK1,829 is the modelled downside, 3.5 times the DKK524 target; "35%" lacks a noun. Risk: reads as a lax or garbled gate. Revise: "continue only if at least 20% of first riders ride again within 90 days and paid media cost per new rider is at most DKK1,829 (downside case); release street screens only at 35% repeat use."

**S5 (C2). "because it owns its cars and employs its drivers."** Evidence: KFST p. 174, already cited, says existing dispatch offices use self-employed operators, not employed drivers. Risk: contrasting only with platforms invites "how does TAXA 4x35 differ?" Revise Drivers of change: "(evolving business models): Uber and Bolt apps book cars run by other operators (p. 119), and existing dispatch offices rely on self-employed operators (p. 174)."

**S6 (C6). "Deferred on purpose: AI chatbots … and a subscription model".** Evidence: the brief lists neuro marketing and revenue model; plan §11 covers both. Risk: trend coverage looks incomplete and AI looks refused. Add: "AI is tested once a human-run FAQ shows which questions recur. Neuromarketing: not used. Revenue model: pay per trip."
