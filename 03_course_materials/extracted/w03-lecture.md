## Slide 3 — Consumer Behaviour

- Key Aspects of Consumer Behaviour:
- Psychological:
- Motivation: Consumers are driven by needs (e.g., buying food when hungry).
- Perception: How consumers view a product (e.g., premium packaging of luxury brands like Louis Vuitton influencing perception of quality).
- Learning: Consumers develop preferences through experiences (e.g., positive experiences with a brand like Apple create brand loyalty).
- Social:
- Culture: Cultural norms and values impact decisions (e.g., fast food preferences in the US vs. healthier food choices in Europe).
- Reference Groups: Influence from family, friends, or celebrities (e.g., followers buying products endorsed by influencers like Kylie Jenner's makeup line).
- Roles: Consumers’ decisions are influenced by their social roles (e.g., a mother purchasing baby products or a professional buying business attire).
- Personal:
- Age: Different age groups have unique preferences (e.g., younger consumers opting for trendy items like streetwear, while older consumers might prioritize comfort and durability).
- Lifestyle: Consumers choose products based on their lifestyle (e.g., fitness enthusiasts choosing gym memberships and sportswear like Nike).
- Personality: Brand choices align with personality traits (e.g., adventurous consumers choosing brands like Jeep for their rugged image).
- Consumer behaviour refers to the processes involved when individuals or groups select, use, and dispose of products, services, or experiences to satisfy their needs and desires.
- Source: Solomon (2022)

## Slide 6 — The Power of Personalisation

- The Power of Personalisation refers to how tailoring marketing messages, product offerings, or digital experiences to individual consumer preferences significantly boosts engagement, satisfaction, and loyalty.
- In the digital age, personalisation has become a strategic tool for businesses to stand out in a saturated market.
- Why It Works:
- Reduces Cognitive Load
- Personalisation helps consumers quickly make decisions by presenting tailored options, reducing the mental effort required to choose.
- Spotify Wrapped offers a year-end summary of listening habits, instantly engaging users with familiar content, reducing the effort needed to discover new music.
- Enhances Emotional Connection
- Personalised experiences create a sense of being understood, which fosters stronger emotional ties to the brand.
- Netflix’s AI-based recommendations offer tailored viewing suggestions based on previous choices, making users feel as though the platform “knows” their preferences, creating a more intimate viewing experience.
- Builds Brand Loyalty
- By offering relevant and personal content or products, brands create a deeper connection, which can lead to repeated purchases or engagement.
- Nike By You allows consumers to design their own shoes, providing a sense of ownership and individuality, which strengthens brand loyalty and encourages repeat purchases.
- Source: Tam & Ho (2020)

## Slide 7 — Social Influence and eWOM (Electronic Word of Mouth)

- Social influence refers to how people’s thoughts, feelings, and actions are affected by others - especially through opinions, recommendations, and behaviours observed in social settings (offline and online).
- In marketing, it impacts consumer decisions through:
- Share A Coke:
- Peer Connection: People were influenced to purchase and share Coke to connect with others socially.
- Social Sharing: It encouraged social media sharing, as consumers posted photos of their personalised bottles, driving further visibility.
- Trend Participation: Consumers were motivated to join the trend as they saw friends and influencers engaging with the campaign.
- eWOM is any positive or negative information about a brand, product, or service shared by consumers online, including:
- Reviews
- Social media posts
- Blogs
- Unboxing videos
- Influencer content

## Slide 9 — Omnichannel Decision-Making

- Consumers use multiple digital and physical channels during their buying journey, combining the benefits of both online and offline experiences.
- Challenges for Brands:
- Consistent Messaging
- Ensuring the same brand tone and message across social media, websites, and in-store experiences. If a brand offers discounts on Instagram but not in-store, it can cause confusion.
- Seamless Transitions
- A consumer may start browsing a product online and then want to continue the shopping journey in-store. Brands need to ensure a smooth experience across both platforms, like a product reserved online for in-store pickup.
- Real-Time Customer Service
- If a customer encounters issues while shopping online or in-store, such as difficulty redeeming a discount, the brand must provide real-time assistance through chatbots or in-store staff to resolve issues quickly and efficiently.

## Slide 10 — Neuromarketing and Emotional Triggers

- Neuromarketing studies how brain responses influence consumer decision-making, with a focus on how emotions shape purchasing decisions.
- Neuromarketing is the application of neuroscience and psychological principles to marketing. It studies how consumers’ brains respond to marketing stimuli -like ads, packaging, brand messages, and product design - by analysing emotions, attention, memory, and decision-making.
- Apple’s minimalist design evokes feelings of simplicity and trust, aligning with its brand identity and making the products more appealing to consumers.
- Coca-Cola’s campaigns leverage happiness and nostalgia (e.g., the "Share a Coke" campaign), triggering positive emotions tied to memories of good times, reinforcing brand loyalty.
- Airbnb uses real stories from hosts and guests, triggering emotional resonance with viewers by highlighting experiences of belonging and adventure, which influences potential customers to book their stays.
