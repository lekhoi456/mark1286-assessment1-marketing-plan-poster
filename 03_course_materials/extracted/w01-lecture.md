## Slide 13 — (no title)

- Sales and Marketing in the Future Economy
  - Sales involves the process of selling products or services, focusing on personalized customer interactions, digital tools, and automation to improve efficiency (Wengler et al., 2021).
  - Sales teams will increasingly use AI, CRM systems, and e-commerce platforms to engage and convert leads, while adapting to remote selling and virtual channels (Harvard Business Review, 2024).
  - Marketing is about creating demand and building brand awareness. In the future, marketing will be heavily influenced by big data, personalized content, and omnichannel (experience across all channels) strategies (Corsaro & Maggioni, 2021).
  - It will emphasize customer-centric approaches, integrating online and offline experiences, and focusing on sustainability and ethical practices to meet rising consumer expectations (Harvard Business Review, 2024).

## Slide 15 — (no title)

- Technological Advancements and Marketing
  - Artificial Intelligence: AI involves using algorithms to simulate human intelligence and improve decision-making. Example: Netflix uses AI to recommend shows based on user viewing history.
  - Personalized marketing: This refers to tailoring marketing efforts to individual preferences and behaviors using data insights. Example: Amazon uses personalized product recommendations based on a customer's browsing and purchase history.
  - Big Data & Analytics Using large volumes of structured and unstructured data to identify patterns, trends, and actionable insights for business decision-making. Example: Coca-Cola uses big data analytics to track consumer preferences across global markets, enabling product innovation and targeted campaigns.
  - Consumer Insights Analysing customer behaviors, preferences, and feedback to understand their needs and enhance marketing strategies. Example: Starbucks collects consumer insights via its loyalty app to personalize offers and improve customer experiences.
  - Targeted Advertising Delivering ads tailored to specific audiences based on demographics, online behavior, or purchase history. Example: Facebook allows businesses to target advertisements using detailed audience segmentation, such as interests and age groups/
- Source: Smith & Jones (2023)

## Slide 16 — (no title)

- The Impact of Technology on Sales
- 1. Digital Transformation in Sales
- E-commerce Platforms: Online platforms that enable businesses to sell products or services directly to consumers. Example: Amazon's platform uses AI to recommend products to customers based on their browsing and purchasing history.
- Virtual Sales Teams and Remote Selling: Sales teams leverage digital tools to engage clients, present solutions, and close deals without needing in-person interactions. Example: Software companies like Salesforce use virtual meeting platforms to conduct remote product demonstrations.
- 2. Salesforce Automation (SFA)
- CRM Tools and Predictive Analytics: CRM tools centralize customer data, and predictive analytics helps forecast sales trends and customer needs. Example: Salesforce CRM combines customer data with analytics to predict purchasing behavior and improve sales targeting.
- 3. Customer Relationship Management (CRM)
- Data-driven Sales Strategies: Using customer data insights to personalize offerings and identify high-potential leads. Example: Retailers analyse purchase patterns to craft personalized discounts or recommendations for frequent buyers.
- Integration of Sales and Marketing Efforts: Aligning sales and marketing teams using shared tools and data to ensure seamless collaboration and unified messaging. Example: HubSpot integrates marketing campaigns with sales pipelines to pass qualified leads directly to the sales team.
- Source: Ali, O. et al. (2023)

- The Role of Data in Consumer Behavior
  - Understanding Consumer Data Behavioral tracking (e.g., browsing patterns) and social media listening help businesses gather insights into consumer preferences and trends. Example: Retailers analyze website browsing patterns to recommend products that align with consumer interests.
  - Consumer Segmentation Businesses divide consumers into groups based on psychographics, demographics, and geographics to create targeted marketing strategies. Example: A travel company develops tailored campaigns for millennials seeking adventure vacations.
  - Personalization Companies deliver tailored experiences to meet individual consumer needs, enhancing customer satisfaction and loyalty. Example: Sephora uses customer purchase history to recommend personalized beauty products through its app.
