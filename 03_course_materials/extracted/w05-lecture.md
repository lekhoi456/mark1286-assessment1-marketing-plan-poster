## Slide 3 — Subscription and Recurring Revenue Models

- Whether a business is planning to introduce recurring payments (fixed or variable) into its model, or is already using them and aims to improve the approach, it is important to distinguish between recurring payments and subscription billing.
- Subscription Models:
- Customers pay a recurring fee to access products or services over time.
- This model provides predictable revenue streams and helps build long-term customer relationships.
- Subscription Billing:
- Refers specifically to the process of charging customers a recurring fee—often upfront—for continued access to a product or service.
- Recurring Billing:
- Emphasizes the repeated payment process, applicable to subscriptions or other regular payments (e.g., utility bills).
- Customers are charged a recurring fee (monthly, annually) for ongoing access to a product or service.
- Provides income at regular intervals, offering financial stability and predictability for businesses.
- Relationship to Subscriptions:
- Subscription models are a subset of the broader recurring payment model.
- Source: Cheng & Zhao (2025)

## Slide 7 — Benefits of Using a Recuring Revenue Model

- ScalabilityRecurring revenue models, especially in digital or service-based businesses, are often highly scalable. As the customer base grows, the business can increase its revenue without necessarily needing to proportionally increase its resources or costs.
- Flexibility for customersThese models can provide greater flexibility and affordability for customers, particularly with models such as subscriptions or memberships. For a predictable fee, customers can access a wide range of products or services that might be prohibitively expensive to purchase outright.
- Opportunity for upselling and cross-sellingBusinesses have more opportunities to offer additional premium services or complementary products, increasing the revenue per customer.
- Increased business valuationBusinesses with recurring revenue are often valued higher than those with transactional revenue models. Predictable revenue can make the business more attractive to investors or buyers.
- Predictability of revenueBusinesses can forecast their revenue more accurately. This improves financial planning and budgeting, providing a valuable sense of stability when planning for growth or navigating market uncertainties.
- Customer retentionA recurring revenue model incentivises businesses to retain their customers, shifting the focus from generating one-off transactions to cultivating long-term relationships. This emphasis on retention often increases customer lifetime value.
- Increased cash flowWith regular payments coming in, businesses benefit from a consistent cash flow. This can significantly improve the financial health of a company and provide funds for reinvestment or growth initiatives.
- Source: Zahay et al., (2022)

## Slide 9 — Challenges and Considerations

- Billing Complexity: Handling upgrades, cancellations, and refunds requires advanced billing systems.
- Revenue Recognition: Timing revenue accurately is challenging, especially with upfront payments and ongoing service delivery.
- Slower Initial Growth: Recurring models often yield slower early revenue, requiring careful financial planning.
- High Customer Service Demand: Continuous support is essential for retention but requires additional resources.
- Retention Effort: Ongoing value delivery (e.g., updates, engagement) is key to prevent churn.
- Price Sensitivity: Regular-fee customers are more reactive to price changes; mismanagement can lead to churn.
- Regulatory Compliance: Businesses must follow rules on transparency and ease of cancellation for subscriptions.
- Source: Cheng & Zhao (2025)
