## Slide 3 — Consumer Behaviour and the Buying Process - Consumer Decision-Making Process (CDMP) (How Consumers Buy a Product)

- 1. Problem Recognition – Realising a Need
- The consumer identifies a problem or need that requires a solution.
- A Zara customer sees a new dress design on Instagram and realises they need a new outfit for an upcoming event.
- 2. Information Search – Online Research, Reviews
- The consumer seeks information to make an informed decision, often through online sources.
- The customer Googles reviews and looks for customer feedback about the dress on different platforms like YouTube or fashion blogs.
- 3. Evaluation of Alternatives – Comparison
- The consumer compares different options to determine which best fits their needs.
- The customer compares the Zara dress to similar styles from other brands (e.g., H&M, ASOS) in terms of price, quality, and design.
- 4. Purchase Decision – Influenced by UX and Trust
- The consumer makes the final decision based on the evaluated alternatives, often influenced by factors like user experience and trust in the brand.
- After finding the best option on the Zara app, the customer proceeds with a purchase due to the app's ease of use and trust in the brand's quality.
- 5. Post-Purchase Evaluation – Reviews, Returns
- The consumer reflects on their decision, possibly sharing feedback or returning the product if it doesn't meet expectations.
- After receiving the dress, the customer posts an outfit photo on social media, and later leaves a review on Zara's website about the fit and quality.
- Source: Engel et al., (1995)

## Slide 8 — Omnichannel Customer Journey Business Marketing Decisions

- Mini Case Study: John Lewis’ Omnichannel Strategy
- John Lewis, a leading UK retailer, has successfully adopted an omnichannel strategy to enhance customer experience both online and in-store. The brand integrates its online store with physical locations, allowing customers to shop seamlessly across channels. Through its Click & Collect service, customers can order products online and pick them up from one of their 500+ stores nationwide, combining the convenience of online shopping with the immediacy of in-store pickup. Additionally, John Lewis uses customer data to personalize offers and recommendations, creating a tailored shopping experience. This strategy has helped John Lewis improve customer loyalty, increase online sales, and drive foot traffic to stores, solidifying its position as a customer-centric retailer in the UK.

## Slide 12 — Activity: ‘The Health-Conscious Fast-Food Search’

- Instructions:
- Form Groups: Split the class into small groups (3-4 students per group).
- Create Your Search Journey:Each group will choose a Millennial or Gen Z persona and answer the following questions about how they would search for the new menu:
  - How do they first hear about the menu? - eWOM(Think about social media posts, influencer reviews, or word of mouth.)
  - Where do they search for more information? - Omnichannel(Would they check Instagram, TikTok, a website, or go directly to the restaurant?)
  - What emotions or visuals make them want to try the new menu? Neuro – Social influence(Consider what makes them feel excited about healthy food – maybe it’s eco-friendly or quick.)
- Scenario:
- A fast-food chain in UK is launching a new health-conscious menu with plant-based, low-calorie, and organic options. They want to attract health-conscious Millennials and Gen Z customers. Your job is to act as a customer and explore how they (you) search for the new menu.
