## Slide 3 — Subscription and Recurring Revenue Models

- [Image alt text: PureGym Generates a 55% Open Rate with Targeted, Relevant… | Braze]
- Subscription: A business model where customers pay a recurring fee (for subscription) at regular intervals (monthly, annually, etc.) to access products or services. The customer commits to a payment cycle for continuous access to the service.
- Recurring Fee (Fixed/Variable): The regular payment made by the customer as part of the subscription model. This fee is billed repeatedly at regular intervals (e.g., monthly) for the duration of the subscription.

## Slide 4 — The Rise of Subscription Models

- Growth Statistics:
- Market Expansion: The global subscription industry surpassed $1 trillion in value in 2020 and is projected to reach $1.5 trillion by 2025, indicating an annual growth rate of approximately 15%. ​
- Revenue Growth: Subscription-based businesses have seen revenues grow 5 to 8 times faster than traditional businesses. ​
- Consumer Adoption: In the United States, 78% of adults have at least one subscription service, highlighting widespread consumer adoption. ​
- Industry Examples:
- Media and Entertainment: Netflix, a leading streaming service, had over 230 million paid subscribers worldwide by the end of 2022, showcasing the success of subscription models in media. ​

- Consumer Goods: Dollar Shave Club disrupted the grooming industry with its subscription model, generating $4 million in revenue in its first year and growing to $240 million within four years before being acquired by Unilever for $1 billion in 2016.

## Slide 5 — Types of Recurring Revenue Models


| Model | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription Model | Customers pay a recurring (monthly/annual) fee for continuous access to a product or service. Ensures predictable revenue and long-term customer engagement. | Netflix – users pay a monthly fee for access to streaming content. |
| Usage-Based (Pay-as-You-Go) Model | Customers are billed based on actual usage of a service. Ideal for users with variable or low usage needs. | Uber – customers are charged based on the distance traveled and time spent during each ride. |
| Freemium Model | Basic features offered for free; premium features require payment. Attracts a large user base, then converts a portion to paid plans. | LinkedIn – users can access basic features such as networking, job searching, and messaging for free. Premium plans, like LinkedIn Premium, offer advanced features such as InMail, additional insights, and enhanced job search filters. |
| Membership Model | Recurring fee paid for access to exclusive benefits, often community-based. Encourages loyalty through perks and exclusivity. | Costco – annual membership grants access to bulk shopping and member-only deals. |
| Retainer Model | Customers pay a fixed recurring fee for ongoing access to professional services. Common in legal, marketing, and consulting industries. | Law Firms – offering monthly legal advisory services for a set fee. |
| License Model | Customers pay a recurring fee to use a product, typically software, for a defined period. Often used in SaaS and enterprise software environments. | Microsoft 365 – per-user, per-month licensing for access to Office apps. |

- Source: Econsultancy (2018)

## Slide 8 — Challenges and Considerations

- [Image alt text: Image result for netflix]
- Mini Case Study:
- Netflix - Managing Recurring Revenue Challenges
- (Read & Discuss)
- Market Challenge: Netflix, as a subscription-based streaming service, faced several challenges typical of recurring revenue models, including billing complexity, revenue recognition, customer retention, price sensitivity, and regulatory compliance.
- Practical Solution: To tackle billing complexity, Netflix implemented automated systems to manage various subscription plans, ensuring accurate billing cycles. For revenue recognition, they adopted ASC 606, enabling them to recognize revenue over time as content was delivered. Customer retention was addressed by personalizing content recommendations and frequently updating their library with new content, while high customer service demand was met with AI-powered chatbots and a self-service platform. Price sensitivity was managed by incrementally raising prices, offering more value with exclusive shows and enhanced features like 4K streaming. Lastly, Netflix ensured regulatory compliance by offering transparent cancellation policies and adhering to data protection standards such as General Data Protection Regulation - GDPR.
- Outcome: By addressing these challenges strategically, Netflix successfully maintained a loyal customer base, managed a predictable revenue stream, and solidified its position as a leader in the streaming industry.

## Slide 9 — Future Trends in Subscription Models

- Peloton Case Study: Future Trends in Subscription Models
- (Read & Discuss)
- Real Market Challenge:
- Peloton saw a drop in demand after the pandemic boom. As gyms reopened, equipment sales fell, and many users canceled their subscriptions, highlighting issues with long-term retention and market saturation.
- Practical Solution:
- Peloton pivoted to a subscription-first model:
- Introduced AI personalization to tailor workouts.
- Expanded into app-only offerings for flexibility.
- Diversified into wellness content (yoga, meditation, outdoor workouts).
- Enhanced community features (leaderboards, live classes) to deepen engagement.
- How They Overcame It:
- They made fitness content accessible without requiring expensive equipment, appealed to broader user preferences through tailored plans, and increased motivation using social and gamified elements. They also formed corporate and healthcare partnerships to reach new audiences.
- Impact:
- Boosted predictable recurring revenue through digital subscriptions.
- Improved customer retention by personalizing the user journey.
- Expanded market reach and repositioned as a tech-driven health and wellness brand.

## Slide 10 — Summary Key points
