# Presentation study guide — Copenhagen, meet Green SM

Date: 5 October 2026. Study companion to [the full script](presentation-script-v01-2026-10-05.md). This is a draft for group review, not evidence of a completed rehearsal or agreed speaking allocation.

## The argument to remember

**Electric + smart + controlled service → a focused local segment → first paid ride → a good experience → repeat use → measured growth.**

The poster is the visual aid. The speech explains the choices, trade-offs and measurements. Learn the argument and the number definitions rather than reading every word in the panels.

## Cue cards by section

### Overview

**Remember:** electric / smart / controlled service; ten panels; trial into repeat.

**Anchor:** Our central proposal is to turn a first paid ride into repeat use.

Explain the reading route once. Do not introduce achieved sales or research that the group has not conducted.

### 1. Company/Business Introduction

**Remember:** Vietnam / March 2023 / Vingroup / app–fleet–drivers / Denmark launch / Netherlands pilot.

**Anchor:** Our plan supports the market-entry period after launch.

Denmark has already launched. Do not describe it as a future market with no service. The flags show international presence, including the explicitly labelled pilot. Do not list the USA as an operating market.

### 2. Target Market Analysis

**Remember:** 25–44 / self-paying AND existing taxi users AND recurring local trips / service area.

**Anchor:** Age alone does not identify a likely customer.

269,277 is the age pool. It is not the qualified segment or a sales forecast. The age range and proposed psychographic profile are choices to validate locally. Existing taxi use reduces the need to test adoption of taxis as a category; recurring trips create a repeat-use test. Neither inference proves this age group is the most profitable.

### 3. Positioning Strategy

**Remember:** electric and app booking are shared / app–fleet–drivers are the operating basis / consistent experience is the proposed benefit.

**Anchor:** Ownership gives control, but does not by itself prove better service than competitors.

State the customer benefit before discussing ownership. Competitors also offer electric options. Do not claim they lack professional drivers or customer support. Cleaner, quieter and more reliable is the company's promise, not a verified comparative result.

### 4. Branding and Identity

**Remember:** corporate identity / Green and Smart Mobility / Go Green For a Green Future / cyan and yellow / Danish first.

**Anchor:** Branding helps customers recognise the offer.

Show the swatches and typeface rather than reading hex codes and Pantone numbers aloud. The group is applying an existing identity locally. It has not designed Green SM's corporate logo or font.

### 5. Digital Marketing Tactics

**Remember:** app habits and price / 4Ps / page → app → ride/help / capped trial / proposed data connection.

**Anchor:** This supports testing a bounded incentive to try another app.

The national sample is 1,005. The separate Capital Region sample is 79, which does not mean Copenhagen municipality. The reasons are multiple-choice responses, so 57% and 22% do not have to sum to 100%. Neither sample is specific to ages 25–44. Do not claim a campaign will convert 57% of viewers.

The search message in the speech is an illustrative execution, not an approved or already-run advertisement. CDP, Big Data and AI reminders are a separate proposed data mechanism, not a confirmed feature of the monthly package.

### 6. Sales Strategies

**Remember:** Discover / First paid ride / Experience / Feedback / Repeat.

**Anchor:** Success means a completed paid ride, not an app download.

The first voucher supports trial. App, driver, vehicle and care support the next purchase. Reviews are unpaid. Monthly offers need frequency and cost testing. The Vietnamese membership precedent does not establish commercial success in Denmark.

### 7. Budget and Resource Allocation

**Remember:** DKK600,000 / twelve months / excluding VAT / delivery first / held spending / cost exclusions.

**Anchor:** This is one shared reserve, not two reserves for the two discount levels.

The team allowance is a planning assumption. 460 hours × DKK600 = DKK276,000. Search and social total DKK198,000. Voucher ceiling is 400 × DKK30 = DKK12,000. M4 and M6 commitments are cumulative, not additive. At M6, DKK265,000 committed + DKK335,000 held = DKK600,000.

Do not imply the marketing budget buys the fleet or that the DKK30,000 tools allocation finances every new AI build. Those operations and unpriced pilots have separate scope. First-year break-even is not required, but contribution still matters for later decisions.

### 8. Measurement and Evaluation

**Remember:** consideration / 1,200 people / 2,500 rides / mature repeat / paid-media cost / service floors.

**Anchor:** DKK165 is not the full marketing budget divided by customers.

Paid search/social spend ÷ distinct paid-attributed first paid riders = the stated acquisition measure. DKK198,000 ÷ 1,200 = DKK165. This confirms the planning arithmetic; it does not independently prove a conversion forecast.

A repeat rate needs complete ninety-day follow-up. Month-six 35% is an interim floor; month-twelve 50% is the later target. Organic and referrals are separate from the paid cohort. Consideration requires its own baseline/endline survey.

### 9. Creativity and Innovation

**Remember:** targeting / dispatch / connected care / packages; measured pilots.

**Anchor:** Innovation earns its place by improving a measured customer or commercial outcome.

Each box has a different purpose. AI dispatch must beat a local comparison on the measures shown, not just match quickly. One trip ID links service touchpoints. A chatbot needs human hand-off. Packages need retention and contribution checks. No local pilot results or completed integrations are claimed.

### 10. Alignment with Business Objectives

**Remember:** stable service / local recognition / green and smart mobility / repeat before expansion / renew–revise–stop.

**Anchor:** The business test is whether customers choose it again at a cost the business can sustain.

Connect the previous nine sections. Avoid introducing another forecast, new strategy or a first-year payback promise in the closing minute.

| Display | Say |

| 25–44 | Twenty-five to forty-four |
| 269,277 | Two hundred and sixty-nine thousand, two hundred and seventy-seven |
| 40.2% | Forty point two per cent |
| n=1,005 | A sample of one thousand and five respondents |
| n=79 | A sample of seventy-nine respondents |
| +10 pp | An increase of ten percentage points |
| DKK600,000 | Six hundred thousand Danish kroner |
| DKK165 | One hundred and sixty-five Danish kroner |
| ≥95% | At least ninety-five per cent |
| ≤2% | No more than two per cent |
| CDP | Customer Data Platform, then C-D-P |
| KPI | Key performance indicator, then K-P-I |
| CRM | Customer relationship management |
| Cohort | A defined group of customers followed over the same observation period |
| Contribution | Amount left after the relevant costs of serving the rides |

A ten-percentage-point increase is different from a ten-per-cent relative increase. For example, moving from 20% to 30% is ten percentage points. This is a teaching example, not the plan's measured baseline.

## Short defence answers for likely questions

| Question | Direct answer |
|---|---|
| Why ages 25–44? | It is a focused starting hypothesis, combined with existing taxi use and recurring trips. Population size supports scale, not superior profitability. Validate locally before expanding the campaign. |
| Is the 57% figure Copenhagen-specific? | No. It is from a Capital Region subsample of 79, with multiple answers allowed. It does not estimate the behaviour of our exact age segment. |
| Why is electric alone not the UVP? | Competitors also have electric options. Our proposed benefit uses direct control to aim for a consistent booking, ride and help experience. Test whether customers value it. |
| Does DKK165 include all marketing costs? | No. It covers search and social spend per distinct paid-attributed first paid rider. Team, screens, tools and vouchers are separate budget lines. |
| Why 1,200 riders? | It is a proposed target consistent with DKK198,000 of paid media at DKK165 per rider. Local tests must establish whether conversion and acquisition cost support that target. It is not an achieved count or guaranteed forecast. |
| Can the budget implement all the AI pilots? | The poster does not price every dispatch, chatbot or package build. They require separate costing, data access and a pilot decision. Do not present them as funded deployments. |
| How is repeat measured? | Follow first paid riders for the full ninety days and measure whether they make another qualifying paid trip. Immature cohorts do not support a ninety-day scale claim. |
| What if the month-six conditions are not met? | Hold the next scale release, diagnose the weak acquisition or service measure, and reassess. If the repeat window is incomplete, postpone the scale decision rather than treating missing follow-up as success. |
| Does year one need break-even? | No. It is an entry investment, but service, retention and contribution still govern whether to renew, revise or stop. |

These are preparation questions, not questions known to have been asked by the lecturer. The group has not yet assigned Q&A owners.

## How to learn and rehearse

1. Learn the one-sentence argument and each section's anchor first.
2. Explain each diagram from its cue words without looking at the full script.
3. Use the script to repair missing reasoning and qualifiers. Do not memorise citations or read URLs.
4. Practise the numbers and the meaning of percentage points, cohort and paid acquisition.
5. Run a timed group rehearsal with pointing and handovers. Record actual times before claiming the pitch fits fifteen minutes.
6. If a section runs long, remove repeated explanation. Keep sample scope, cost scope and proposed-target qualifiers.
