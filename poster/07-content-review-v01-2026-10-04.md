## Verified existing arithmetic

Source inputs: ../02_research/marketing-plan/budget-scenario-inputs.json, selected_s1; calculation semantics: ../06_workflow/scripts/model_marketing_scenarios.py. No new external benchmark is claimed.

- Search: DKK108,000 / assumed DKK12 CPC × 3% = 270 attributed first paid riders.
- Social: DKK90,000 / assumed DKK90 CPM × 1,000 × 1% CTR × 1.5% conversion = 150 attributed first paid riders.
- Deduplication: (270 + 150) × 90% = 378 unique first paid riders.
- Extra rides: 378 × assumed 75% with a full 90-day window × 35% repeat × three extra rides = 297.675.
- Modelled rides: 378 + 297.675 = 675.675, displayed approximately 676.

This is a conditional paid-acquisition cohort calculation. It does NOT measure total Copenhagen fleet rides, SEO/organic acquisitions, brand-led demand or all post-90-day trips. It is also not an incremental-lift experiment. There is no evidence allowing unmeasured channel demand to be added. Treating 676 as the overall annual market plan forecast was too broad.

The voucher cap limits promoted first rides, not the total number of customers. The DKK12,000 reserve remains a single ceiling; maximum DKK30 for up to 400 riders, one first paid ride per rider. More riders do not silently increase the reserve.

## Evidence-backed weaknesses in the existing plan

1. The old gates lack a numeric customer/ride-volume commitment. Under the illustrative media assumptions, M4 produces 63 unique first paid riders from DKK33,000 media, and M6 produces 147 from cumulative DKK77,000 media.
2. If the M3–4 test spend is evenly split by month, the March cohort is approximately 31.5 new riders. Only that early cohort can have completed 90 days by end June. A percentage review needs its cohort count and observation window, not only a threshold.
3. The DKK1,829 paid-acquisition ceiling reproduces the downside scenario. At assumed DKK227 fare and 30% contribution, roughly 26.9 rides are needed to recover that acquisition spend alone, before vouchers and fixed marketing delivery. Passing this gate does not demonstrate economically supported growth.
4. Existing 75% cohort maturity, repeat frequency, CPC/CPM/conversion and margin inputs are assumptions. A complete annual forecast needs monthly acquisition cohorts and actual trip frequency, net receipts and operating contribution.
5. Allocation totals reconcile, but empirical feasibility remains unproven. A larger decorative trip target cannot repair this.

## Concrete revised targets for discussion — not a forecast

A more demanding candidate is 1,200 unique new paid riders and 2,500 completed paid rides from those customers during M1–12. It is a stretch target rounded below the existing upside scenario, not a company forecast or proven attainable demand. It covers marketing-acquired customers, not total fleet activity. Annual paid-media cost per first rider <=DKK165 follows DKK198,000 / 1,200. Annual 90-day repeat target 50% would replace the existing 35% target and therefore needs explicit acceptance and dependent Section 8/10 amendments. With 1,200 first riders, assumed 75% mature, 50% repeat and three extra rides, the conditional arithmetic is 2,550 rides. This does not prove the monthly acquisition schedule delivers those rides within the year.

Candidate M4 review: >=200 unique first completed paid riders; service checks retain completion >=95%, service-caused cancellation <=2%, 24h complaint response >=90%. DKK33,000 paid spend / 200 = DKK165 per first rider. The upside assumptions would give approximately 210.46 first riders at this stage.

Candidate M6 review: >=450 cumulative unique first paid riders; observed paid-media cost per first rider <=DKK175; repeat >=35% on a full 90-day cohort to authorise screens. DKK77,000 / 450 = DKK171.11; the upside assumptions give approximately 491.08 cumulative first riders. A proposed minimum of 100 fully observed first riders for the repeat review requires confirmation; it is an operational minimum, not a claim of statistical precision. If the cohort is not mature/large enough, defer the release rather than calling it a failed retention result.

DKK175 is a proposed test-stage cap close to the annual DKK165 acquisition requirement, not an external benchmark. All reviews also require service capacity and observed contribution/retention to justify spending. A financial gate is not satisfied merely because an acquisition target is achieved.
