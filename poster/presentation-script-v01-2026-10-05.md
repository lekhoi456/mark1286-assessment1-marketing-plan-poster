# Copenhagen, meet Green SM — presentation script

## Overview
<!-- speaker: Speaker A -->
<!-- time: 0:45 -->

[Point to the title, then trace the three rows. Read the speech, not these directions or the citations. Speaker A/B/C are provisional roles for the group to allocate.]

Our poster presents a twelve-month market-entry plan for Green SM's local electric taxi service in Copenhagen. The plan connects three strengths: electric vehicles, smart booking and direct control of service delivery.

The upper row explains the company, customers, positioning and identity. The middle row shows how we attract customers, sell rides, fund the activities and measure results. The final row explains innovation and business growth.

Our central proposal is to turn a first paid ride into repeat use. The figures are proposed targets, not achieved results.

## 1. Company/Business Introduction
<!-- speaker: Speaker A -->
<!-- time: 1:00 -->

[Point to the app, taxi and flags.]

Green SM is a Vietnamese electric mobility brand, founded in March 2023 and backed by Vingroup. It combines app booking with an owned fleet and employed drivers (Green Future USA Inc., no date c, pp. 2–3; Konkurrence- og Forbrugerstyrelsen, 2026b, p. 174).

The flags show international presence. Vietnam is the home market, Denmark our focus, and the Netherlands a pilot (Green SM, 2026a, p. 1).

Green SM officially launched in Copenhagen on 30 July 2026. Our plan therefore supports the market-entry period after launch, with the aim of establishing local demand and repeat usage. The service in scope is Green SM Taxi: electric taxi rides booked through the app (Green SM, 2026d, pp. 1–2).

## 2. Target Market Analysis
<!-- speaker: Speaker A -->
<!-- time: 1:30 -->

[Point to the highlighted 25–44 bar, the selection connector and the rider profile.]

Our priority is Copenhagen residents aged 25–44 who pay personally, already use taxis and make recurring local trips within the service area. These conditions work together. Age alone does not identify a likely customer.

The population data gives us an age pool of 269,277 people, or 40.2 per cent of all city residents. That is the population opportunity, not the number of customers we expect to acquire (Statistics Denmark, 2026b, pp. 6–8).

We prioritise existing taxi users to test switching to Green SM. Recurring trips give us an opportunity to measure repeat behaviour. The proposed rider profile adds three needs: value for money, control of the trip and fair treatment.

The population chart does not prove that 25–44 is the most profitable group. The age choice is a starting hypothesis. During the first two months, local research should test these needs before wider spending. The reason for this narrow starting segment is to learn who returns after a first ride.

## 3. Positioning Strategy
<!-- speaker: Speaker A -->
<!-- time: 1:15 -->

[Compare both columns, then point to the direct-control bracket.]

Our positioning is: electric rides, one managed experience.

Electric vehicles and app booking are already offered by competitors. DRIVR offers electric rides through Uber and its own app, while Viggo's Bolt announcement also describes an electric service. Those features establish parity, so they cannot carry our differentiation alone (DRIVR, no date, p. 2; Viggo, 2025, p. 2).

Green SM's owned app, owned fleet and employed drivers give it a way to manage standards across the journey. We propose turning that operating model into a customer benefit: a consistent experience from booking to the ride and after-sales help (Konkurrence- og Forbrugerstyrelsen, 2026b, p. 174).

The company's cleaner, quieter and more reliable promise supports this direction. Reliability still needs local measurement. Ownership gives control, but does not by itself prove better service than competitors (Green SM Denmark ApS, no date c, p. 2).

## 4. Branding and Identity
<!-- speaker: Speaker A -->
<!-- time: 1:00 -->

[Point to the logo, slogan, colour swatches and app illustration.]

We retain Green SM's corporate identity. Green SM stands for Green and Smart Mobility, and the slogan is Go Green For a Green Future.

The poster shows the New Cyan and New Yellow palette, Xanh Display 2.0 and the Modern Liquid Glass app direction. These elements come from the existing identity, rather than a separate Copenhagen redesign (Aurora Vietnam and InSpace Creative, 2026).

Our local adaptation is Danish first and English second. The same recognisable identity should appear on the campaign page, in the app and across service touchpoints.

Branding helps customers recognise the offer. Service must then support the electric and smart positioning.

## 5. Digital Marketing Tactics
<!-- speaker: Speaker B -->
<!-- time: 2:00 -->

[Start with the two survey blocks, then follow page → app → ride and the voucher.]

The customer evidence points to price and app habits. In a national Danish sample of 1,005 respondents, 40–50 per cent reported having one taxi app installed. In a separate Capital Region sample of 79, 57 per cent selected lower price and 22 per cent their usual app as reasons for their last platform-app choice. Multiple answers were allowed. These are different samples, and neither proves the behaviour of Copenhagen residents aged 25–44 (Konkurrence- og Forbrugerstyrelsen, 2026b, pp. 150–151, 194).

This supports testing a bounded incentive to try another app. The 4Ps connect the offer: electric rides and service, a clear fare and trial offer, page-to-app booking, and promotion through search, social and reminders.

Our omnichannel journey keeps fares, offers and help consistent from the Copenhagen page to the app and the ride. An illustrative search message could be: Electric taxi in Copenhagen. Check your fare and book in the app.

We propose testing 10 versus 20 per cent off the first paid ride, capped at DKK30, with at most 400 riders and one offer each. Repeat-use packages are separate paid pilots.

With opt-in, a Customer Data Platform would join customer records. Big Data analysis could identify patterns for human-reviewed AI reminders. This is proposed integration, not a confirmed local system (Customer Data Platform Institute, no date).

## 6. Sales Strategies
<!-- speaker: Speaker B -->
<!-- time: 1:30 -->

[Trace Discover → First paid ride → Experience → Feedback → Repeat.]

Marketing creates interest. The sales process turns that interest into completed, paid trips.

At Discover, search and social generate leads. A fare check develops interest into a booking prospect. The first-ride offer supports conversion, but success means a completed paid ride, not an app download.

The next stage is Experience. The app, driver, vehicle and customer care should deliver the same standard. If that experience disappoints, another voucher cannot repair the problem on its own.

After the ride, we ask for feedback and a rating. We resolve problems and invite unpaid reviews. We do not buy favourable reviews.

Repeat use is supported by opt-in reminders and tested offers. A tailored monthly package would need suitable trip frequency and workable costs. Green SM has introduced membership offers in Vietnam, but that establishes a precedent for the mechanism, not proof that the offer will succeed in Denmark (GSM Green and Smart Mobility Joint Stock Company, 2024b, p. 1).

## 7. Budget and Resource Allocation
<!-- speaker: Speaker B -->
<!-- time: 2:00 -->

[Point to the total, largest bars, hours and M4/M6 reviews. Do not read every row.]

The proposed marketing budget is DKK600,000 for twelve months, excluding VAT.

Team resources are the largest allocation at DKK276,000. The planning allowance is 460 hours at DKK600 per hour, covering checks, creative localisation, campaigns, and marketing CRM and help coordination. Search receives DKK108,000 and social DKK90,000, giving DKK198,000 for paid acquisition.

The remaining allocations fund screens, tools, the first-ride voucher test and contingency. The voucher ceiling is DKK12,000: 400 riders multiplied by a maximum DKK30 each. This is one shared reserve, not two reserves for the two discount levels.

Spending is released through reviews. By month four, cumulative commitments are DKK177,000. By month six, they are DKK265,000, leaving DKK335,000 held for later decisions. Those review figures are cumulative, so we do not add them together.

The budget prioritises local delivery and testing before wider visibility. It excludes cars, drivers, charging and frontline operations. New dispatch, chatbot and subscription pilots need separate costing. The plan does not treat those builds as already funded by the tools allowance.

Our year-one objective is a controlled entry investment. First-year break-even is not required, but costs and repeat behaviour must still guide further spending.

## 8. Measurement and Evaluation
<!-- speaker: Speaker C -->
<!-- time: 1:45 -->

[Point to the five target cards, then the service and review rows.]

The twelve-month targets cover consideration, paid trial, usage, retention and acquisition cost. We propose 1,200 distinct first paid riders, 2,500 paid rides from the acquired group, at least 50 per cent repeat within ninety days among riders with full follow-up, and paid acquisition cost no higher than DKK165 per rider.

DKK165 means search and social spending divided by distinct first paid riders attributed to those campaigns. DKK198,000 divided by 1,200 gives DKK165. It is not the full marketing budget divided by customers.

Consideration should increase by ten percentage points between the month-one-to-two baseline survey and month twelve, using the same target definition. Impressions do not measure that change.

Service floors are at least 95 per cent completion, no more than 2 per cent service-caused cancellations and at least 90 per cent help within twenty-four hours.

Month four requires at least 200 first paid riders. Month six requires 450 cumulatively, acquisition cost no higher than DKK175 and at least 35 per cent repeat in fully observed ninety-day cohorts. Incomplete follow-up postpones the scale decision. Organic and referral demand are tracked separately.

## 9. Creativity and Innovation
<!-- speaker: Speaker C -->
<!-- time: 1:30 -->

[Point to each of the four pilot boxes.]

The four innovation pilots address different problems.

AI targeting would use consented customer data to make reminders more relevant. AI dispatch would test whether matching improves against a local baseline, checking fulfilment and cancellations alongside matching time. A faster match is not useful if service quality falls.

Connected care would use one trip identifier across the app, driver or vehicle, and support. Ratings and customer-care records could reveal where the journey breaks down. An FAQ chatbot would hand unresolved problems to a person.

Personalised packages would match actual trip frequency. We would compare pay-per-trip with prepaid or monthly offers, checking retention and contribution. Contribution means the amount left after the relevant costs of serving those rides. The pilot stops if retention or margin falls.

These are proposed, locally untested mechanisms. Their funding and data access need to be established before deployment. Innovation earns its place by improving a measured customer or commercial outcome.

## 10. Alignment with Business Objectives
<!-- speaker: Speaker C -->
<!-- time: 0:45 -->

[Point to the four business goals, then return to the whole poster.]

The plan supports four goals: stable local operations, local recognition, green and smart mobility, and sustainable growth.

Marketing generates trial. Service and customer care support repeat use. Resources and measurement control when we expand.

At month twelve, the decision is to renew, revise or stop, based on consideration, paid trial, service, mature repeat behaviour and contribution.

Copenhagen, meet Green SM is an invitation to try the service. The business test is whether customers choose it again at a cost the business can sustain.
