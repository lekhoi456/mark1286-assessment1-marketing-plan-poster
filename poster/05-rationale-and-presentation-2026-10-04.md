## Why retain these elements?

| Element | Strategic role / delivery point |
|---|---|
| App and app-choice evidence | An incumbent app is a switching barrier; price motivates some surveyed app choices. Use the figures directionally, retaining different sample/geography/question and the age-specificity limit. They do not establish conversion behaviour of Copenhagen residents aged 25–44. |
| Applied 4Ps | Connect the electric/service offer, clear fare and trial, app distribution and promotion. The framework organises decisions; its name alone is not analytical evidence. |
| Illustrated channel journey | Paid search captures active taxi intent; SEO answers fare/area/booking questions; social content demonstrates the electric ride and app booking. All direct to the local page then app, ride and help with consistent terms. Channel roles are proposed rationale, not verified local campaign performance. |
| Trial voucher and 30-day paid package | The bounded first-ride offer tests initial switching; the proposed package addresses repeat usage. Vietnam evidence supports implemented mechanics, not proven Danish success or a quantified commercial uplift. Keep the single DKK12,000 reserve ceiling in Section 7. |
| CDP / Big Data / Marketing AI | Proposed join of trip/app/consent records, repeat-pattern identification and relevant reminder timing/message. Opt-in, human review and data/system readiness remain prerequisites. A local deployment or achieved uplift is not claimed. |
| Three-stage timeline | Verify service/data, test channels/offers at M4/M6 gates, then refine/scale conditionally. Budget release and exact evaluation thresholds belong in Sections 7/8. |

## Suggested delivery order — about 75–90 seconds, not timed

1. Point to the insight bars; explain the switching problem and the limits of the samples.
2. Follow the illustrated journey; give one practical example for each tactic: paid search “electric taxi Copenhagen”, SEO fare/area/booking FAQs, social short ride-and-booking demonstration.
3. Point to the voucher and calendar; explain first-ride trial versus repeat use, caps and the proposed package.
4. Point to the data flow/timeline; explain relevant opt-in reminders, human review, staged testing, then hand over to the sales journey, budget and dashboard. Evaluate redemption and paid repeats; clicks alone do not establish commercial success.

## V04 insight labels and data separation

Denmark n=1,005 identifies the national taxi-user responses to the installed-app question. Capital Region n=79 identifies the regional last-platform-app-choice subsample, not Copenhagen municipality. Multiple responses were permitted: the same person could select lower price and habitual app use, so the reason bars are separate and are not a 100% allocation. These differing question/base limits and lack of a 25–44-specific estimate remain in presentation notes after the surface wording is removed. The DATA lane is a separate proposed customer-data/reminder mechanism; it is not a feature annotation of the 30-day paid package.
