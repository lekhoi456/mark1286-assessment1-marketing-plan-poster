## Part A — Official criteria and weights (verbatim)

The `C1`–`C7` IDs are our stable cross-reference keys; they are not printed in the source. The seven rows after the ID column are verbatim.

| Local ID | Assessment Criteria | Weightage (%) | Description |

| C2 | 2. Target Market, Segmentation & Branding | 15% | Target market(s) are well-defined using demographic, psychographic, and behavioural characteristics. Branding elements (e.g., logo, message) are creative and aligned with the target audience. |

**Weight check (editorial):** 10 + 15 + 20 + 15 + 10 + 10 + 20 = **100% of A1**, which contributes 50% of the module mark. These are not seven equal shares. The source does not allocate separate marks to the eleven content headings or publish a separate pitch/Q&A weighting.

## Part B — Proposed operational tests (not official bands)

Use **pending / evidenced / gap / needs clarification / not applicable with reason** in dated draft reviews. A checked test means evidence was inspected, not that a marker awarded credit. Record the draft locator and evidence key for every judgement. Do not use a tick count to calculate a grade. All tests start pending: no business, poster, pitch or contribution account is approved yet.

### C1 — Company Background and Country Context (10%)

- [ ] **C1.1** The company/business is named and its background, country of origin, operating context and core products/services are clear. Existing-business facts have verified evidence; hypothetical-business choices are labelled as proposals.
- [ ] **C1.2** Context explains a consequential marketing choice rather than presenting disconnected history. The introduction is concise enough to locate quickly on the poster.

### C2 — Target Market, Segmentation & Branding (15%)

- [ ] **C2.1** The target segment is described through demographic, psychographic and behavioural characteristics; the method of segmentation and the priority choice are explicit and evidenced or labelled assumptions.
- [ ] **C2.2** A positioning statement and a relevant unique value proposition distinguish the offer from named, evidenced alternatives. Comparative claims are not invented.
- [ ] **C2.3** Logo, colour scheme and brand message form a coherent identity aligned with the target audience and positioning. Distinguish an existing identity from proposed changes; explain why the creative choices fit.

### C3 — Digital Marketing & Sales Strategies (20%)

- [ ] **C3.1** Each proposed digital channel has a target-audience reason, a concrete tactic/example and a role in the strategy. Social media, content, SEO and other platforms are considered for fit rather than merely listed.
- [ ] **C3.2** Sales channels and techniques explain how leads are generated, prospects developed and conversions achieved. The sales process links to the digital activity and the revenue/customer-acquisition objective.
- [ ] **C3.3** The proposal is feasible given the planned people, tools, budget and time. Distinguish current company practice from the group's recommendation; example content does not masquerade as an executed campaign.

### C4 — Budget & Resource Allocation (15%)

- [ ] **C4.1** A readable chart or table gives a stated total, currency, period and allocations; amounts sum to the total and shares sum to 100%. Avoid overlapping percentage ranges presented as an allocation.
- [ ] **C4.2** Prices and quantities have a source or a clearly labelled planning assumption and reproducible calculation. A revenue statistic is not substituted for a marketing budget.
- [ ] **C4.3** Money and other resources cover the actual proposed activities; the allocation is realistic and its trade-offs can be defended. No invented company budget is presented as observed fact.

### C5 — KPIs & Success Metrics Aligned with Objectives (10%)

- [ ] **C5.1** Each KPI is linked to a named marketing objective and the relevant business objective; the measure evaluates the proposed strategy rather than only reporting available metrics.
- [ ] **C5.2** Each KPI states a target, tracking tool/method and review rhythm. Targets and any baselines are sourced or explicitly labelled assumptions, with no fabricated historical performance.
- [ ] **C5.3** Evaluation explains how results would inform action; long-term sustainability and growth are addressed rather than asserted. Proposed review decisions are not described as decisions the group already took.

### C6 — Creativity & Use of Emerging Concepts (10%)

- [ ] **C6.1** An original, business-specific approach applies relevant emerging concepts to a customer or commercial problem. Explain the mechanism and intended benefit, not just a trend name.
- [ ] **C6.2** The brief's longer trend list is considered, with selection or non-selection justified by fit. This is our relevance-based reading, not an official waiver requiring no lecturer clarification.
- [ ] **C6.3** Concepts are traceable to module sources; any outside concept is flagged and PDF-backed. Evidence, assumptions and practical/ethical limitations of the proposal are distinguishable. Slide reference lists are not treated as verified bibliographies.

### C7 — Visual Appeal & Presentation Quality (20%)

- [ ] **C7.1** All eleven canonical headings can be located; information hierarchy, colour and spacing support a clear reading route. Meaningful graphs, charts, symbols and visuals explain the strategy rather than crowding it.
- [ ] **C7.2** The actual A0 proof and submitted image are legible, including citations and identifiers. Check the physical viewing distance and the digital image separately; zooming into a photograph is not proof of print legibility.
- [ ] **C7.3** Names and student numbers are correct; contribution wording reflects only confirmed work. Evidence of all members' effort and active presentation participation is real, not inferred from three names on a poster.
- [ ] **C7.4** The original hand-drawn requirement remains visible in the compliance review. A0 is a student-relayed lecturer instruction; the AI-made hand-drawn-style production route was agreed by the lecturer (relayed by the student, LG-002, D-021). The proof must still read as deliberately hand-drawn, colourful and well organised; agreement to the route is not a mark for the result.
- [ ] **C7.5** Rehearsal covers a 15-minute pitch and five minutes of questions, with all three members speaking. The argument, visuals and answers are consistent with the approved plan; recording a proposed speaker split is not evidence of rehearsal.

### Cross-cutting release checks — local IDs X1–X6

- [ ] **X1** Every source-dependent poster claim has a credible source and an in-poster citation; the complete reference list is included in the agreed physical and digital locations. Resolve “back” versus “end” rather than assuming the front image is sufficient.
- [ ] **X2** Every cited work has a verified, registered PDF; website snapshots are dated. Cite Them Right Harvard, 13th edition (2025), is used consistently under D-004. No invented claims, sources or source locators.
- [ ] **X3** Each student uploads the same clear poster through their own Moodle account and retains a receipt. Turnitin subject details include the tutorial room, date/time and tutor's name. Verify the local upload route and accepted file format before submission.
- [ ] **X4** Confirm the pitch date and the Can Tho submission date/time; use 14 October 2026 as the handbook deadline, not the old brief's 17 June date. Confirm tutor-approved roster, group identifier and the applicability of T5's composition instruction.
- [ ] **X5** Group decisions and real contributions are confirmed and logged. The agent drafts; the group decides. No business, targets, budget or member work is treated as settled before confirmation.
- [ ] **X6** Approved Markdown remains the text master; final design preserves it exactly. English (UK) is used outside verbatim quotations, and the research/review apparatus stays behind the scenes.
