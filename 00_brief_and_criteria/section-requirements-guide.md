# Section requirements guide — Assessment 1

Prepared 3 October 2026 at the student's request. This is an explanation of the supplied assessment sources and the group's existing plan, not new official instructions, approved poster wording or a draft review.

Authority: [source brief](brief.md), especially handbook §7, PDF pp. 7–10; [official rubric](rubric.md), Part A. The source text copies are in `source/`. The practical outputs below are our interpretation of how to answer the questions; they do not create new marking bands.

## What the assignment assesses

Develop a comprehensive marketing strategy for a real or hypothetical business, summarise it on a poster and pitch it. Apply course concepts to the business. LO1 concerns emerging trends, technologies and customer behaviour; LO2 concerns effective marketing and sales strategies aligned with globalisation trends.

There are **at least eleven content elements**, **seven weighted rubric criteria** and cross-cutting presentation/reference/submission requirements. The eleven headings do not have eleven separate mark allocations. Presentation Quality applies across the artefact; it is not a twelfth substantive marketing section.

## The eleven sections

### 1. Student/Business Details

**Explicit requirement:** registration-system details and business name; the opening instructions also require all members' names and student numbers and contributions, if applicable.

**Practical output:** the three confirmed names/numbers, the business name and a truthful contribution statement. Adding the plan's service, focus market and period makes its scope clear. Contribution wording must come from the group; listing names does not establish completed work.

### 2. Company/Business Introduction

**Explicit requirement:** business background, operating locations/context and products/services. Rubric C1 also specifies country of origin.

**Practical output:** a concise, sourced introduction that establishes the organisation, its offer and the relevant country context. For this plan, distinguish the Vietnamese company background from the Copenhagen service being marketed. Keep dated company facts separate from recommendations.

### 3. Target Market Analysis

**Explicit requirement:** identify and describe target markets; give demographic, psychographic and behavioural characteristics; explain segmentation and prioritisation.

**Practical output:** show the segments considered, the selected segment, its three required characteristics and the reason for prioritising it. Geographic scope can sharpen the analysis. The approved priority is S1: adults in the verified Copenhagen service area who already take some recurring taxi trips and pay themselves (D-032). An illustrative persona is a planning device, not survey evidence.

### 4. Positioning Strategy

**Explicit requirement:** a clear positioning statement, differentiation from competitors and a unique value proposition relevant to the audience.

**Practical output:** state who the offer serves, its customer benefit and why the proposed difference is credible relative to named alternatives. The approved P1 concerns clear local taxi service and a route to resolution; its appeal remains a hypothesis (D-032). Naming an electric drivetrain alone does not establish uniqueness.

### 5. Branding and Identity

**Explicit requirement:** logo, colour scheme and brand messaging, aligned with positioning and the target market.

**Practical output:** show the chosen identity and explain why it expresses P1 for S1. Distinguish Green SM's existing identity from proposed campaign material. D-033 approves Danish-first communication. D-037 replaces the displayed English campaign line with “Clear terms. Local care.” The brief does not explicitly require an existing business to receive a completely new corporate logo.

### 6. Digital Marketing Tactics

**Explicit requirement:** selected digital channels/tactics for reaching the audience; explain customer engagement through social media, content marketing, SEO and other platforms.

**Practical output:** connect each chosen channel to an audience need, a concrete tactic/example and a role in the customer journey. The approved mix includes search, local page/SEO, social and CRM (D-033). A list of platform names does not explain the strategy.

### 7. Sales Strategies

**Explicit requirement:** sales channels and techniques that drive revenue and customer acquisition; explain lead generation, prospecting and conversion.

**Practical output:** show how the interested person progresses to a completed paid ride, which channel completes the sale and what helps conversion. In the approved consumer plan, explain the app-booking journey. A completed paid ride is a commercial outcome; an app install is an earlier step.

### 8. Budget and Resource Allocation

**Explicit requirement:** realistic implementation budget and allocation across channels/activities. Rubric C4 requires an organised visual budget, such as a table or pie chart, and feasibility.

**Practical output:** a table/chart showing currency, period, total, line items and resources needed to deliver the tactics. Our workspace additionally requires amounts to reconcile and percentages to total 100%, with sourced costs or labelled assumptions. DKK600,000 over 12 months and the integrated allocation are approved (D-032–D-033); these are planning figures, not observed company expenditure.

### 9. Measurement and Evaluation

**Explicit requirement:** success metrics/KPIs and how campaign performance will be tracked and analysed. Rubric C5 requires alignment with marketing objectives.

**Practical output:** link each KPI to an objective, state how it is measured and explain the resulting review action. Our agreed working standard adds a target, tracking method/tool and review rhythm to every KPI. Approved targets remain planning assumptions; they are not measured Green SM results.

### 10. Creativity and Innovation

**Explicit requirement:** creativity in the marketing approach and incorporation of emerging trends. The longer prompt names AI, personalisation, omnichannel, neuromarketing, subscription and revenue models. Rubric C6 rewards effective application and originality.

**Practical output:** explain the customer/commercial problem, the proposed mechanism and its intended benefit. D-033 approves personalised trip reminders and one trip reference across channels, with AI chatbot and subscription deferred. Describe the revenue model and the treatment of the other named trends. The sources do not state a numerical quota or explicitly say to implement every named trend. Considering the whole list and justifying selection/deferment is our interpretation, not a lecturer-issued exemption.

### 11. Alignment with Business Objectives

**Explicit requirement:** explain alignment with overall business goals and consider long-term sustainability and growth potential.

**Practical output:** connect business goals to marketing objectives, tactics, funded resources and outcome measures. Explain conditions for maintaining or expanding the initiatives, including financial and delivery limits. The approved plan is a capped market-entry investment with release gates; all previously modelled cases fall short of contribution break-even. Do not recast these scenarios as positive ROI or proven company losses.

## How the official marks are allocated

| Official criterion | Weight within A1 |
|---|---:|
| Company Background and Country Context | 10% |
| Target Market, Segmentation & Branding | 15% |
| Digital Marketing & Sales Strategies | 20% |
| Budget & Resource Allocation | 15% |
| KPIs & Success Metrics Aligned with Objectives | 10% |
| Creativity & Use of Emerging Concepts | 10% |
| Visual Appeal & Presentation Quality | 20% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

A1 contributes 50% of the module mark. Positioning and business alignment remain required even though they do not each have a separately named weighted criterion. The current sources do not publish a distinct poster-versus-pitch percentage split.

## Boundaries and presentation requirements

- SWOT, PESTLE, Porter's Five Forces and the 4Ps are not explicitly required as separate panels in the supplied brief/rubric. Any analytical framework used must answer a relevant requirement and follow the module-first rule.
- No numeric word limit is stated in these sources. Approximately 1,400 words was the earlier group direction (D-034); D-035 supersedes it as a fixed content cap, prioritising complete assessable content and infographic conversion, still subject to actual A0 legibility.
- The original separate brief states A1 paper; the locally agreed working size is A0 (D-007), landscape (D-034). The lecturer's agreement to the AI-made hand-drawn-style route is recorded through the student (D-021). These decisions do not alter the verbatim source.
- Pitch: 15 minutes, followed by 5 minutes of questions; every member participates. Each member submits the same poster through their own Moodle account.
- Use relevant graphs/tables and credible in-poster citations with a complete reference list. Physical/digital reference placement still needs confirmation.
- Budget sums, KPI target/tool/rhythm detail, registered source PDFs and the concept/evidence registers are workspace verification rules. They support the assignment but are not additional official mark allocations.
